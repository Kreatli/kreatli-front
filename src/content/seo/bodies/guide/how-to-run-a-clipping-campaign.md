# How to Run a Clipping Campaign: CPM Campaigns vs. Hired Clippers

**How to run a clipping campaign** comes down to one choice first: pay many clippers per 1,000 views to post your clips on their own accounts (a CPM campaign), or hire dedicated clippers at a fixed rate to produce clips for your own channels. A CPM campaign needs a budget, a rate per 1,000 views, payout caps, minimum view thresholds, clear rules, a content kit, and a verification process. A hired-clipper campaign needs a scope, a job post, and a reliable editor.

This guide covers how to set up each, and when to use one, the other, or both.

## Two kinds of clipping campaign

| Factor | CPM campaign | Hired clippers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who posts | Clippers, on their own accounts | You, on your own channels |
| What you pay for | Views (per 1,000) | Finished clips (per clip, project, or retainer) |
| Cost predictability | Capped by budget, variable per result | Fixed and known upfront |
| Control over output | Low: many clippers, varied quality | High: one editor learns your style |
| Main benefit | Reach across many accounts quickly | Consistent growth of owned channels |
| Main work for you | Rules, review, view verification | Briefing and feedback |

For a definition of the campaign format, see [what is a clipping campaign](/glossary/what-is-a-clipping-campaign).

## Setting up a CPM campaign

CPM campaigns usually run on a content rewards platform or through a community such as a Discord server. Platforms differ in fees and features, so check their current terms. As of 2026, Whop's Content Rewards, for example, has brands set a rate per 1,000 views, fund a budget, and optionally set a minimum and maximum payout per video, then approve or reject submissions. The steps below apply to most setups.

### 1. Set the budget

Decide the total you are willing to spend. Your budget is the hard ceiling on cost. Divide it by your rate to see the maximum paid views it can buy:

- $2,000 budget ÷ $1 per 1,000 views = **2,000,000 paid views** maximum

Run numbers for different rates in the [clipping campaign cost calculator](/tools/clipping-campaign-cost-calculator).

### 2. Choose a rate per 1,000 views

Campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**. A higher rate attracts more and better clippers but spends your budget faster. A lower rate stretches the budget but may draw fewer skilled participants. Match the rate to how attractive your footage is: well-known creators with strong moments can often pay less than unknown brands.

### 3. Add a payout cap

A **payout cap** limits what one clip can earn so a single viral video does not drain your whole budget. If your rate is $1 per 1,000 views and your cap is $300, a clip stops earning at 300,000 views. Caps protect breadth; set one that still makes a big win worthwhile for the clipper. See [payout cap meaning](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning).

### 4. Set a minimum view threshold

A minimum keeps your review queue focused. If clips must reach 2,000 views before they qualify, you skip reviewing hundreds of low-reach posts. Make the threshold clear in your rules so clippers know what counts.

### 5. Write the rules

Clear rules reduce disputes. Include:

- **Allowed platforms** (TikTok, Shorts, Reels, X)
- **Required elements:** your handle in captions, a hashtag, a link, or an on-screen credit
- **Banned content:** misleading titles, reuploading full episodes, offensive edits, AI voiceovers if you do not want them
- **Clip length and format**
- **Disclosure:** clippers should label paid promotion where platform rules or local law require it
- **Submission window** and campaign end date
- **What gets rejected** and why

### 6. Build a content kit

Give clippers what they need to make good clips fast:

- Source footage links or a shared folder
- A list of suggested moments with timestamps
- Logo, fonts, and caption style if you want consistency
- Two or three example clips you like
- Talking points or a call to action

A good kit raises average quality because clippers spend time editing rather than searching.

### 7. Verify views before paying

View-based campaigns attract some fake engagement. Before approving payouts, check analytics evidence, engagement ratios, and account history. Consider a short holding period so views settle before payment. The full process is in [how to verify clip views](/guides/how-to-verify-clip-views).

### 8. Review and iterate

After the first week, look at which moments and hooks performed, then update your kit and suggested timestamps. Raise or lower the rate depending on participation.

## Setting up a hired-clipper campaign

If you want clips on your own channels, a "campaign" is really a production pipeline.

1. **Define the scope:** source content, clips per week, platforms, style, turnaround.
2. **Set the pay:** typically **$15–$35 per clip** for talking-head or podcast edits and **$50–$75+** for complex gaming or motion work, or a monthly retainer.
3. **Post the job:** on Kreatli Marketplace, list the deliverables, pay type (hourly, per project, per month, or per year), and availability.
4. **Vet and test:** shortlist profiles, review samples, and run a paid test clip.
5. **Onboard:** share a style guide, templates, and a handoff process.
6. **Post and measure:** you publish clips and track performance in your own analytics.

Because you pay per deliverable, there is no view verification step. A clip that underperforms still costs the agreed rate, but you never pay for inflated views. The [step-by-step guide to hiring a clipper](/guides/how-to-hire-a-clipper) covers each stage in detail.

## When to use each

**Use a CPM campaign when:**

- You are launching something and want **wide, fast distribution**
- Your content has many clippable moments that work for cold audiences
- You can spend time on rules, review, and verification
- You accept that quality and messaging will vary across accounts

**Hire dedicated clippers when:**

- You want to **grow your own channels** consistently
- Brand voice and quality control matter
- You want a **predictable monthly cost**
- You need someone who learns your content over time

## Combining both

Many creators run both at once. A hired clipper produces a steady stream of polished clips for your owned channels, and a CPM campaign spreads your best moments across other accounts during launches or key moments. The hired clipper can even help: their best-performing clips make strong examples for your campaign content kit.

A simple combined plan:

- Hire one clipper for 15 clips per week on your channels
- Run a capped CPM campaign during a launch month
- Feed top-performing moments from your channel into the campaign kit

Start the owned-channel side by [posting a clipping job](/jobs/create). For a deeper comparison of the two models, see [Kreatli vs. Whop Clips](/compare/kreatli-vs-whop-clips).

## FAQ

### How much budget do I need for a clipping campaign?

There is no fixed minimum, but divide your budget by your rate per 1,000 views to see how many paid views it can buy. For example, $1,000 at $1 per 1,000 views covers up to 1,000,000 paid views. Hiring a clipper instead costs a fixed amount per clip or per month.

### What is a good CPM rate for a clipping campaign?

Campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**. Higher rates attract more clippers but spend the budget faster. Pair your rate with a payout cap and a minimum view threshold to control spend.

### How do I stop people from botting views in my campaign?

Require analytics access or screen recordings rather than screenshots, check engagement ratios against views, look at account history, and hold payouts until views settle. Reject clips that show sudden unnatural spikes with no engagement.

### Can I run a clipping campaign and hire a clipper at the same time?

Yes. A hired clipper builds your owned channels with consistent output, while a CPM campaign adds reach across other accounts. They solve different problems and work well together.
