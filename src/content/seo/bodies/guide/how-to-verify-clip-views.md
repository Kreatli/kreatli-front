# How to Verify Clip Views and Spot Botted Clipping Results

**Clipping view verification** means confirming that the views a clipper reports are real, human views before you pay for them. In CPM campaigns, where clippers earn per 1,000 views, some participants inflate numbers with bots or bought engagement. The most reliable checks are direct analytics access or screen recordings, engagement ratios that make sense for the view count, a clean account history, and a holding period before payout.

This guide covers each check, the warning signs of botted views, and how to set rules that discourage fraud in the first place.

## Why view verification matters

In a CPM campaign, your budget converts directly into views. Every fake view is money paid for nothing. Botted results also distort your learning: if you think a certain hook got 300,000 views when it really got 8,000, you will make the wrong creative decisions next time.

Verification is a cost of running view-based campaigns. If you are new to them, read [how to run a clipping campaign](/guides/how-to-run-a-clipping-campaign) first, and see [verified views meaning](/glossary/verified-views-meaning) for how platforms use the term.

## Screenshots vs. analytics access

Not all proof is equal.

| Evidence type | Reliability | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screenshot of view count | Low | Easy to edit; shows nothing about audience quality |
| Public view count on the post | Medium | Real number, but cannot show whether views are bought |
| Screen recording of native analytics | Medium–high | Harder to fake; shows retention, traffic sources, geography |
| Platform-tracked views via a campaign tool | Medium–high | Depends on how the platform detects fraud; check their terms |
| Direct analytics access or live screen share | High | You see the dashboard yourself in real time |

**Avoid paying on screenshots alone.** If your campaign platform tracks views automatically, still spot-check large payouts. If it does not, ask for a screen recording that starts on the account profile and navigates to the clip's analytics, so the account and numbers are shown in one continuous take.

## Signals of botted or fake views

No single signal proves fraud. Look for several together.

### Engagement that does not match views

Real viewers like, comment, share, and save at some rate. A clip with 200,000 views and a dozen likes is a red flag. Compare each clip against the **same account's other posts** and similar clips in your campaign rather than a universal benchmark, since ratios vary by platform and niche.

### Unnatural view curves

Organic clips usually grow in waves as the algorithm tests them. Warning signs include:

- A sudden spike of tens of thousands of views in minutes, then flat
- Views that rise steadily at a perfectly even pace
- Views that jump right before a campaign deadline

### Odd traffic sources and geography

Native analytics show where views come from. Be cautious when:

- Almost no views come from the For You feed or Shorts feed, yet totals are high
- Most views come from countries unrelated to your content's language or audience
- Traffic comes mainly from external or unknown sources

### Very low retention

If analytics show average watch time of a second or two on a 30-second clip while views are huge, the views may not be from real people. Learn what normal looks like in [video retention rate meaning](/glossary/video-retention-rate-meaning).

### Generic or repetitive comments

Bot comments tend to be short, generic, and repeated across posts: single emojis, "nice video," or comments in unrelated languages. Real comments reference the content.

### Suspicious account history

Check the posting account:

- Brand new account with sudden high view counts
- Follower count that does not fit the reach
- Past posts with wildly inconsistent numbers
- Many accounts in your campaign with similar names or identical posting patterns

## Holding periods

A **holding period** is a delay between a clip's submission and its payout, for example 7–14 days. It helps because:

- Platforms sometimes remove fake views after detection, so numbers can drop
- Deleted or private posts can be excluded
- You have time to review large earners before money moves

Announce the holding period in your campaign rules so honest clippers know what to expect. Some platforms build a hold into their payout process; check their current terms.

## Rules that prevent fraud upfront

Prevention is cheaper than investigation. Put these in your campaign brief:

- **Proof requirement:** analytics screen recording or platform tracking, not screenshots
- **Right to reject:** you can reject clips with suspicious metrics, without payout
- **Payout caps:** limit what one clip can earn so a botted clip cannot drain your budget (see [payout cap meaning](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning))
- **Minimum account standards:** for example, account age or posting history
- **Posts must stay live** for a set period to qualify
- **Ban on bought engagement:** state that any purchased views, likes, or follows disqualify the account

Apply rules consistently. Clippers talk to each other, and fair enforcement keeps good participants coming back.

## FAQ

### How do you verify views in a clipping campaign?

Ask for direct analytics access or a continuous screen recording of the clip's native analytics, not a screenshot. Check that engagement, retention, traffic sources, and geography make sense for the view count, and hold payouts for a set period before paying.

### How can you tell if views are botted?

Common signs include very low likes and comments relative to views, sudden spikes followed by flat lines, traffic from unrelated countries, extremely short average watch time, generic repeated comments, and new accounts with outsized reach. Look for several signals together rather than relying on one.

### Are screenshots enough proof of clip views?

No. Screenshots are easy to edit and show nothing about whether views are real. Use screen recordings that move from the profile to the clip analytics, platform-tracked views, or live dashboard access instead.

### Does Kreatli verify clip views?

Kreatli does not track or verify views. On Kreatli Marketplace you hire clippers for agreed pay per clip, project, or period, and you post the clips on your own channels, so there are no third-party view counts to verify.

### What is a holding period in clipping campaigns?

It is a delay between submission and payout, for example 7–14 days, that lets views settle and gives you time to review large earners. Platforms may remove fake views during that window, so payouts reflect more accurate numbers.
