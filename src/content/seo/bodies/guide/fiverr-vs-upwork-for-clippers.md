# Fiverr vs Upwork for Clippers: Where to Find Clipping Work

When comparing **Fiverr vs Upwork for clippers**, the short answer is this: Fiverr suits packaged one-off clip offers, Upwork suits proposal-driven contracts that may be hourly or fixed price, and a clipping-specific marketplace like **Kreatli Marketplace** suits clippers who want creators that already know what a clipping job is. Many clippers use more than one, but one usually becomes the main source of repeat clients.

This guide compares the three from a clipper's point of view: who the buyers are, how you get work, how pay works, and where each one breaks down.

## Quick comparison

| Factor | Fiverr | Upwork | Kreatli Marketplace |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Focus** | General freelance gigs | Broad freelance contracts | Clipping and short-form editing |
| **How you get work** | Buyers order your packaged gig | You send proposals to job posts | You apply to clipping jobs creators post |
| **Buyer intent** | Mixed, many one-off orders | Mixed, many non-clip editing jobs | Creators looking for clips specifically |
| **Pay structure** | Per order, set by your gig tiers | Hourly or fixed price | Set by the creator: per project, hourly, monthly, or yearly |
| **Payments** | Processed by the platform, with a fee taken from earnings | Processed by the platform, with a service fee | Arranged directly between creator and clipper |
| **Competition** | High, global, often price-driven | High, with a proposal grind | Smaller pool, all clip-focused |

Fee structures on large platforms change over time, so check each platform's current pricing page before you set your rates. Creator-side comparisons are in [Kreatli vs Fiverr](/compare/kreatli-vs-fiverr) and [Kreatli vs Upwork](/compare/kreatli-vs-upwork).

## Fiverr for clippers

Fiverr works on a storefront model. You publish a gig with packages, and buyers order from it.

**Works well when:**

- You need a **first review** quickly
- You can sell a crystal-clear package, such as "5 podcast clips, 48-hour delivery"
- You are comfortable competing in a crowded, global marketplace

**Breaks down when:**

- Buyers expect unlimited revisions at the lowest tier
- A buyer wants 30 clips a week but orders one basic package
- Platform fees push you to rush work to keep your hourly rate reasonable

Fiverr trains buyers to think in single orders. Turning a one-off buyer into a weekly client takes deliberate effort.

## Upwork for clippers

Upwork works on a proposal model. Clients post jobs, and you spend Connects to submit proposals.

**Works well when:**

- A client posts a **well-scoped** clipping contract with clear deliverables
- You already have a profile with completed jobs and good feedback
- The project mixes long-form and short-form work with clear milestones

**Breaks down when:**

- You spend Connects on vague "need video editor" posts that are not really clipping jobs
- A client wants hourly tracking for work that should be priced per clip
- Generalist editors bid low on niche work

Upwork rewards proposal volume and profile history, which makes the first few contracts the hardest.

## Kreatli Marketplace for clippers

Kreatli Marketplace is built around one job: creators (streamers, YouTubers, podcasters) hiring clippers to turn long content into Shorts, TikToks, and Reels for the creator's own channels. Clippers sign up free, build a profile with samples, and apply to [open clipping jobs](/clipping-jobs).

**Works well when:**

- You specialize in **podcast, Shorts, TikTok, gaming, or stream** clipping
- You want **fixed pay** for finished clips instead of view-based earnings
- You want to build **recurring work** with a few creators

**Breaks down when:**

- Your portfolio has no vertical short-form samples
- You expect work to arrive without applying or keeping your profile current
- You want to post clips on your own accounts and earn per view, which is a different model

Because Kreatli does not process payments, you and the creator agree on the payment method and timing yourselves. Get that in writing before you start, the same way you would with any direct client.

## A practical starting strategy

1. **Build 3–5 vertical samples** in one niche. The [portfolio guide for editors with no clients](/guides/video-editing-portfolio-no-clients) shows how.
2. **Set your rates.** Simple clips typically pay **$15–$35**, and complex gaming or motion edits **$50–$75+**. See [how to price video editing services](/guides/how-to-price-video-editing-services).
3. **Make one platform your main focus.** For clipping work, start where buyers are already looking for clips.
4. **Add a second channel selectively.** A tight Fiverr gig can help you collect a first review, and well-scoped Upwork posts can be worth a few proposals.
5. **Review after 10 completed projects.** Put your time where repeat clients show up.

## How to stand out as a new clipper

On any platform, the same habits win work:

- **Lead with niche samples,** not a general showreel
- **Apply with specifics,** such as "10 clips per episode, 48-hour delivery, two revision rounds"
- **Treat the first batch as an audition** and deliver early if you can
- **Ask for a review** after each completed job

For outreach ideas beyond marketplaces, read [how to find your first video editing client](/guides/find-first-video-editing-client).

## Which should you choose?

**Choose Fiverr** if you want to sell a fixed package and can handle a price-driven marketplace.

**Choose Upwork** if you also want broader editing contracts and have time to write proposals.

**Choose Kreatli Marketplace** if clipping is your main skill and you want creators who already speak hooks, captions, and 9:16. When you are ready, [create a free clipper profile](/signup/professional) and apply to jobs that match your niche.

## FAQ

### Is Fiverr or Upwork better for clippers?

It depends on how you like to sell. Fiverr suits clippers who want to package a fixed offer and let buyers order it. Upwork suits clippers who prefer pitching on individual job posts. Both have large pools of general editing work, so clippers often add a clipping-specific marketplace to reach creators who want clips specifically.

### Can I use Fiverr, Upwork, and Kreatli at the same time?

Yes. Keep the same niche and the same samples across all of them so buyers see a consistent specialist. After a few months, focus on the platform where repeat clients actually come from.

### Which platform has the lowest fees for clippers?

Compare your **net pay per clip**, not the headline rate. Include platform fees, proposal costs, and time spent on revisions. A higher rate with less friction often nets more than a cheaper, high-volume gig.

### Does Kreatli pay clippers per view?

No. Creators on Kreatli Marketplace post jobs with pay they set: per project, hourly, monthly, or yearly. You deliver finished clips and the creator posts them. Per-view clipping campaigns, which pay roughly $0.50–$3 per 1,000 views, are a separate model.
