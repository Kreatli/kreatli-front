# Whop Clipping: How It Works, What It Pays and How to Start

**Whop clipping** means cutting short clips from a brand's or creator's long videos, posting them on your own TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels or X account, and getting paid for the views. It runs through Whop's Content Rewards campaigns. Each campaign sets a rate per 1,000 views, and you only earn on clips the campaign approves. Across clipping platforms, rates commonly sit around **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**, so most clips earn a few dollars and a few big ones earn most of the money.

This guide covers how a campaign works, the maths, what cuts into earnings, and how it compares with fixed-pay clipping jobs.

## How Whop clipping works

Whop's own documentation (checked October 2026) describes Content Rewards like this. A business sets up a campaign, and clippers do the rest:

1. **Pick a campaign.** Each one lists its rate per 1,000 views, its budget and the platforms it accepts.
2. **Read the brief.** The campaign gives you the source content and the rules: length, captions, hashtags, what to avoid.
3. **Cut and post the clip.** You edit a short vertical clip and post it on your own account.
4. **Submit the link.** You send the post to the campaign for review.
5. **Get approved or rejected.** The business checks whether the clip followed the rules. Only approved clips get paid.
6. **Earn on the views.** After approval, Whop pays you based on the views the clip got.

The business funds the budget and pays the clippers. For the model in one page, see [what content rewards are](/glossary/content-rewards-meaning).

## What Whop clipping pays

You earn the campaign's rate for every 1,000 views on an approved clip. If you're new to the term, read [what CPM means in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping).

| Views on an approved clip | At $0.50 per 1,000 | At $1 per 1,000 | At $3 per 1,000 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5,000 | $2.50 | $5 | $15 |
| 50,000 | $25 | $50 | $150 |
| 500,000 | $250 | $500 | $1,500 |

Some campaigns add a **flat fee bonus** on top. Whop's docs give this example: at a $3 rate with a $10 flat fee, a clip with 2,000 views earns $16 ($6 from views plus $10).

A more typical month looks uneven. Say you post 20 clips to a campaign paying $1 per 1,000 views with a 2,000-view minimum. Fourteen get under 2,000 views, so they earn nothing. Five get 10,000 views each and earn $50 together. One gets 150,000 views and earns $150. That's $200 for 20 clips, and three quarters of it came from one clip.

## What cuts into earnings

The rate is only part of the story. These rules decide how much of your work gets paid:

- **Approval.** A clip that breaks the brief gets rejected and earns $0, however many views it got.
- **Minimum payout.** Campaigns set a minimum a clip must earn before it's even reviewed. Whop's docs give the example of a $3 rate with a $6 minimum, which means a clip needs about 2,000 views. See [minimum view thresholds](/glossary/minimum-view-threshold-meaning).
- **Maximum payout.** A cap limits what one clip can earn. In Whop's example, a $3,000 cap at a $3 rate stops earnings at 1 million views. See [payout caps](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning).
- **Budget.** When the campaign's budget runs out, it stops paying. Submitting late can mean earning nothing.
- **Views that don't count.** Campaigns pay on views they accept. Views that look fake or bought can be removed. See [verified views](/glossary/verified-views-meaning).

## Is Whop clipping legit?

Yes. Content Rewards is a real Whop feature, and businesses pay real money for approved views. Watch for these around it:

- **Paid courses.** Many "how to make money with Whop clipping" guides sell a course or an editing tool. You don't need either to start.
- **Income claims.** Posts about beginners making thousands a month are outliers. Most clips earn little.
- **Fake campaigns.** Don't pay a fee to join a campaign, and don't send personal details outside Whop.

For a full checklist, read [is clipping legit](/guides/is-clipping-legit).

## Do you need followers?

Not to join, but reach matters a lot. You post on your own accounts, so your earnings depend on how many people see your clips. A new account with no history often gets few views, which means many clips won't reach the minimum.

Fixed-pay clipping work is different. You deliver the files and the creator posts them on their channels. Your samples matter, not your follower count.

## Whop clipping vs fixed-pay clipping jobs

| | Whop clipping | Fixed-pay clipping job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How you're paid | Per 1,000 views on approved clips | An agreed price per clip, batch or month |
| Typical pay | About $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views | $15 to $35 per simple clip; retainers about $800 to $3,500+ a month |
| Where clips go | Your own accounts | The creator's own channels |
| Followers needed | They help a lot | No, just good samples |
| How steady | Low: most clips earn little | High: the price is agreed upfront |
| Upside | A viral clip can pay well | Capped at the agreed rate |

Many clippers do both. Fixed-pay clients give a steady base, and campaigns add upside. Keep them apart: clips you make for a client belong on the client's channels. ## How to start

1. **Set up your accounts.** Pick one or two platforms the campaigns accept, such as TikTok or YouTube Shorts. Post regularly so the accounts have some history.
2. **Get a free editor.** CapCut is enough to start. See [the best editing software for clippers](/guides/best-video-editing-software-for-clippers).
3. **Choose campaigns you can win.** Look for a fair rate, a low minimum, budget still left, and source content you'd actually watch.
4. **Read every rule.** Most rejections come from skipped details, like a missing hashtag or a watermark from another app.
5. **Post often and track results.** Note which hooks, lengths and topics clear the minimum. Do more of those.
6. **Build a portfolio as you go.** Your best clips double as samples for fixed-pay jobs.

## FAQ

### Is Whop clipping legit?

Yes. Whop Content Rewards is a real feature where businesses pay clippers for approved views. Be careful with paid courses and income claims around it, never pay a fee to join a campaign, and read each campaign's rules before you post.

### How much does Whop clipping pay per 1,000 views?

Each campaign sets its own rate. Across clipping platforms, rates commonly sit around $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views. At $1 per 1,000, a clip with 50,000 approved views earns $50.

### Do you need followers to make money clipping on Whop?

You can join without followers, but you post on your own accounts, so reach drives earnings. New accounts often get few views at first, and clips under a campaign's minimum earn nothing.

### Why was my Whop clip rejected?

Usually because it broke a campaign rule. Common reasons are the wrong length, missing captions or hashtags, a watermark from another app, or a clip that reposts the source without editing it. Read the brief again before your next submission.

### What does CPM mean on Whop?

CPM means cost per mille, or the pay per 1,000 views. On Whop, the campaign's reward rate is its CPM. A $1 rate means you earn $1 for every 1,000 views on an approved clip.

### Can I clip on Whop and take fixed-pay jobs at the same time?

Yes. Many clippers use fixed-pay clients for steady income and campaigns for extra upside. Keep client work separate, since clips made for a client belong on the client's channels.
