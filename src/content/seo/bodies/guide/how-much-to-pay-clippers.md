# How Much to Pay Clippers: Per-Clip, Retainer, and CPM Rates

**How much to pay clippers** depends on the model you choose. If you hire a freelance clipper to edit clips for your own channel, you will typically pay **$15–$35 per clip** for simple talking-head or podcast edits and **$50–$75+ per clip** for complex gaming or motion graphics work. If you run a CPM campaign where clippers post on their own accounts, campaigns commonly pay roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**, with caps and minimum view thresholds.

This guide covers each pricing structure, what moves rates up or down, and sample budgets. To model your own numbers, try the [clipping campaign cost calculator](/tools/clipping-campaign-cost-calculator).

## Choose your model first

Before you set a rate, decide what you are paying for.

- **Hiring a clipper** means you pay for **editing work**. The clipper delivers finished files and you post them on your own channels. Your cost is fixed and known in advance.
- **Running a CPM campaign** means you pay for **views on other people's accounts**. Clippers edit and post your footage; you pay based on performance. Your cost scales with results and is capped by your budget.

Both are legitimate. Hiring builds your owned channels with consistent output. CPM buys distribution across many accounts. Many creators combine them. The [guide to running a clipping campaign](/guides/how-to-run-a-clipping-campaign) compares the two in detail.

## Per-clip rates

Per-clip pricing is the most common starting point when you hire.

| Clip type | Typical rate |
| --- | --- |
| Talking-head or podcast clip with captions | $15–$35 |
| Clip with B-roll, zooms, and branded captions | $30–$50 |
| Gaming highlight with SFX and memes | $50–$75+ |
| Motion graphics or heavily designed clip | $75+ |

Rates go up when the clipper has to **find the moments** in raw footage rather than edit timestamped segments you provide. A four-hour stream with no notes takes much longer than a 20-minute segment with three marked timestamps.

**Example:** You want 15 podcast clips per week at $25 each.
15 × $25 = **$375 per week**, or about **$1,500 per month** over four weeks.

## Retainer rates

Once you know you need clips every week, a monthly retainer is usually easier for both sides. You agree a volume and a fixed monthly fee.

**Example retainer:**

- 20 clips per week (80 per month)
- Agreed effective rate of $22 per clip
- 80 × $22 = **$1,760 per month**

Retainers often come with a slightly lower per-clip rate than one-off work because the clipper gets predictable income and less time spent finding clients. In return, define clearly:

- Weekly volume and what happens to unused clips
- Turnaround per batch
- Number of revision rounds included
- Platforms and export formats
- What counts as out of scope (thumbnails, long-form edits, posting)

On Kreatli Marketplace you can post jobs with **per-month or per-year pay** for retainer-style arrangements, or **per-project and hourly** pay for trials and one-off batches. The [gig vs. project vs. retainer glossary entry](/glossary/gig-vs-project-vs-retainer) explains the trade-offs.

## Hourly rates

Hourly pay works for open-ended tasks like reviewing a back catalog, building templates, or mixed editing. It is harder to budget for pure clip output, so most creators convert to per-clip or retainer pricing once the workflow is stable. If you pay hourly, agree an estimated hours cap per week.

## CPM rates for campaigns

If you run a CPM campaign instead of hiring, you set a **rate per 1,000 views**, a **total budget**, and usually two guardrails:

- **Minimum view threshold:** clips below it earn nothing, which keeps low-effort submissions out of your review queue
- **Payout cap per clip:** limits how much one viral clip can take from your budget

**Example CPM campaign:**

- Budget: $1,000
- Rate: $1 per 1,000 views
- Cap: $200 per clip
- At $1 per 1,000 views, $1,000 buys up to **1,000,000 paid views** before the budget runs out

Read [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping) and [payout caps](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning) if these terms are new. With CPM you do not control which clips go out or which accounts post them, so your cost per useful result is less predictable than a fixed hire.

## What affects how much you should pay

- **Complexity of the edit:** captions only vs. B-roll, SFX, motion graphics
- **Source material:** timestamped segments vs. raw VODs
- **Niche knowledge:** gaming, finance, and technical topics reward clippers who understand the content
- **Volume:** higher, steady volume justifies a lower per-clip rate
- **Turnaround:** 24-hour delivery costs more than a weekly batch
- **Revisions:** unlimited revisions should cost more than one round
- **Experience and portfolio:** proven clippers with strong samples charge more and usually need less direction

Paying at the very bottom of the range often costs more later through rework, missed deadlines, or clips that do not match your voice.

## Hidden costs to budget for

- **Revision rounds.** Agree how many are included, usually one or two per clip. Extra rounds should cost extra.
- **Source file access.** A tidy Dropbox or Google Drive folder saves your clipper time; a messy library costs you.
- **Music licensing.** Royalty-free music is standard; premium tracks may be a separate line item. See [royalty-free music for clips](/glossary/royalty-free-music-for-clips).
- **Platform fees.** Generalist gig sites take a cut from one or both sides. Compare total cost in [Kreatli vs. Fiverr](/compare/kreatli-vs-fiverr) and [Kreatli vs. Upwork](/compare/kreatli-vs-upwork).
- **Scaling up.** Past about 30 clips a week, compare freelancers with a [full-time in-house editor](/compare/freelance-clipper-vs-in-house-editor) or a [clipping agency](/compare/clipping-agency-vs-clipping-marketplace).

## How to pay fairly and avoid disputes

- **Start with a paid test clip** at your normal rate rather than asking for free samples.
- **Write the scope down** in your job post and first message: volume, style, turnaround, revisions.
- **Agree payment timing and method** before work starts. Kreatli does not process payments, so you and the clipper agree how and when to pay (for example bank transfer, PayPal, or card).
- **Use milestones** for larger batches so neither side carries all the risk. See [milestone payments](/glossary/milestone-payment-freelance).
- **Review rates every few months** as volume and quality change.

When you are ready, [post a clipping job](/jobs/create) with your pay type, rate range, and deliverables. Clear numbers attract clippers who fit your budget. For more on finding the right person, see [how to hire a clipper](/guides/how-to-hire-a-clipper).

## FAQ

### How much should I pay a clipper per clip?

Typically **$15–$35** for simple talking-head or podcast clips and **$50–$75+** for complex gaming, motion graphics, or heavy SFX work. Pay more when the clipper reviews raw footage to find the moments, delivers fast, or brings niche expertise.

### What is a fair retainer for a clipper?

Multiply your monthly clip volume by a per-clip rate in the typical range, then adjust slightly down for guaranteed volume. For example, 80 clips per month at $22 is $1,760. Spell out turnaround, revisions, and what counts as out of scope.

### How much do clipping campaigns pay per 1,000 views?

CPM clipping campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**, usually with a minimum view threshold and a payout cap per clip. Your total spend is limited by the budget you fund. Check the platform's current terms for any fees on top.

### Is it cheaper to hire a clipper or run a CPM campaign?

It depends on your goal. Hiring is cheaper and more predictable if you want consistent clips on your own channels. CPM can be efficient for spreading a launch across many accounts, but you pay for views rather than control over output. Many creators use both.
