# How to Hire a Clipper: A Step-by-Step Guide for Creators

To **hire a clipper**, decide how you want to pay (per clip, per project, or retainer), define exactly what you need each week, write a clear job post, vet portfolios, run a paid test clip, then onboard your pick with a simple repeatable workflow. Done well, the process takes a week or two and gives you a clip editor who learns your style and delivers finished shorts for your own channels.

This guide walks through each step. If you already know your format, jump to the [hire clippers hub](/hire) for platform-specific hiring pages.

## Step 1: Decide which model you want

"Clipper" means two different things online, so start here.

- **Hired clipper:** a freelance editor who turns your long videos, streams, or podcasts into Shorts, TikToks, and Reels, and **delivers the files to you**. You post on your own channels and pay a fixed rate.
- **CPM campaign clipper:** someone who edits your footage and **posts on their own account**, earning per 1,000 views from a campaign budget.

This guide is about the first model. If you want distribution across many accounts instead, read [how to run a clipping campaign](/guides/how-to-run-a-clipping-campaign).

Then pick a pay structure:

| Structure | Best when |
| --- | --- |
| Per clip | You are testing or volume changes week to week |
| Per project | You have a defined batch, such as one podcast season |
| Hourly | The work is open-ended, like reviewing a back catalog |
| Monthly retainer | You need steady weekly output and want consistency |

For rate ranges and sample budgets, see [how much to pay clippers](/guides/how-much-to-pay-clippers).

## Step 2: Define the scope

Most bad hires start with a vague scope. Before you post anything, write down:

- **Source content:** podcast episodes, Twitch VODs, YouTube videos, webinars
- **Weekly volume:** for example, 15 clips per week from two episodes
- **Platforms:** YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, or all three
- **Clip length:** for example, 20–45 seconds
- **Style:** caption style, fonts, zooms, B-roll, memes, music
- **Who finds the moments:** you provide timestamps, or the clipper reviews full footage
- **Turnaround:** 24 hours, 72 hours, or a weekly batch
- **Revisions:** how many rounds are included
- **Extras:** titles, hooks, thumbnails, or captions for posting

Collect three to five reference clips you love, from your channel or others. References communicate style faster than any description.

## Step 3: Write the job post

A strong job post filters out mismatched applicants before you spend time on them. Include:

1. **A specific title:** "Podcast clipper for 15 vertical clips per week" beats "Video editor needed"
2. **What you make and who watches it**
3. **Deliverables and volume**
4. **Style references** (links)
5. **Turnaround and revision expectations**
6. **Pay type and rate or range**
7. **Availability:** full-time, part-time, or project-based
8. **How to apply:** ask for two or three relevant samples and a short note on how they would approach your content

On Kreatli Marketplace you can set availability, pay type (hourly, per project, per month, or per year), and your preferred payment methods directly in the job, so applicants know the terms before they write a cover letter.

## Step 4: Vet applicants

Shortlist three to five clippers. For each, check:

- **Relevant samples:** a gaming editor and a podcast editor make different choices; look for your niche
- **Hooks:** does the first second give a cold viewer a reason to stay?
- **Moment selection:** are the clips actually the best parts, or just the loudest?
- **Captions:** readable, accurate, and not covering faces or gameplay UI
- **Audio:** clean levels, no clipping on loud reactions
- **Communication:** clear, prompt, and specific replies to your questions
- **Reviews:** feedback from past creators, if available

Clipper profiles on Kreatli are reviewed by the Kreatli team, which saves you some screening, but you should still judge fit yourself. The [guide to vetting a freelance video editor](/guides/how-to-vet-a-clipper) has a fuller checklist.

## Step 5: Run a paid test clip

Give your top two or three candidates the **same short source segment** and the same brief. Pay your normal per-clip rate. Free tests attract the wrong people and waste good clippers' time.

Compare the results on:

- Which moment they chose and why
- Hook strength in the first few seconds
- How closely they matched your references
- Turnaround versus what they promised
- How they handled feedback on a small revision

Often the difference between candidates is judgment, not technical skill. Hire for judgment.

## Step 6: Onboard your clipper

The first two weeks set the pattern. Give your new clipper:

- **A style guide:** fonts, caption colors, logo placement, banned words or topics
- **A templates folder:** caption presets, intro or outro cards, music guidance
- **A handoff format:** where source files go, how you mark timestamps, where finished clips land
- **A feedback channel:** one place for notes so nothing gets lost
- **Context:** recurring jokes, sensitive topics, sponsors to avoid cutting badly

Agree payment method and timing in writing. Kreatli does not process payments, so you and the clipper settle how you pay (bank transfer, PayPal, card, or another method you both accept) and when.

## Step 7: Manage the relationship

Treat your clipper like a production partner.

- **Batch feedback** weekly instead of sending scattered notes
- **Share performance** so the clipper learns which hooks and topics work for your audience
- **Keep a simple tracker** of clips requested, delivered, and posted
- **Review scope and rates** every few months as volume grows
- **Leave a review** when a job completes; it helps the clipper and the next creator

If volume grows past what one person can handle, add a second clipper for a separate format or platform rather than overloading the first.

## FAQ

### Where can I hire a clipper?

You can hire clippers on dedicated clipping marketplaces like Kreatli Marketplace, general freelance sites, or through referrals and creator communities. A clipping marketplace focuses on short-form clip editors, so applicants usually already understand vertical formats, hooks, and captions.

### How much does it cost to hire a clipper?

Freelance clippers typically charge **$15–$35 per clip** for simple talking-head or podcast edits and **$50–$75+** for complex gaming or motion work. Retainers for steady weekly volume often land at a slightly lower per-clip rate.

### Should I give a clipper a test before hiring?

Yes, and pay for it. A paid test clip on the same source segment lets you compare candidates on moment selection, hooks, and how they take feedback. It is the most reliable way to judge fit before committing to weekly work.

### What should I include in a clipper job post?

Include your content type, weekly volume, platforms, clip length, style references, turnaround, revision rounds, pay type, and how to apply. Specific posts attract clippers who fit and filter out generic applications.

### Does the clipper post the clips for me?

On Kreatli Marketplace, the clipper delivers finished files to you and you post them on your own channels. That keeps control of your accounts, analytics, and audience with you. If you want a clipper to also schedule or post, agree that explicitly in the scope.
