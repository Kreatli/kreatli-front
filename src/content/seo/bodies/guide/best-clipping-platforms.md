# Best Clipping Platforms in 2026: Where to Get Paid to Clip

The **best clipping platforms** in 2026 fall into two camps. CPM campaign platforms like Whop Content Rewards, Vyro, ClipFarm, and ClipAffiliates pay per 1,000 views on clips you post to your own accounts, while fixed-pay options like Kreatli Marketplace, Fiverr, and Upwork pay for the edit itself, whatever the views.

This guide is mainly for clippers, with a short section for creators. Rates and fees come from each platform's public pages or named reporting as of 2026, and terms change often, so confirm them first.

## How clipping platforms pay

Almost every option uses one of two models:

- **CPM campaigns.** A brand or creator funds a budget and sets a rate per 1,000 views. You cut clips, post them on your own TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, or X accounts, and submit the links. Your pay depends on views, minimum thresholds, per-clip caps, and whether the budget lasts. See [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping).
- **Fixed pay.** A creator hires you to edit clips and posts them on their own channels. You are paid per clip, per hour, or on a retainer, and a clip that flops still pays.

For current market ranges in both models, see the [clipper pay rates](/guides/clipper-pay-rates) reference.

## The platforms

### Whop Content Rewards

**How it pays:** CPM. Per Whop's documentation, the business funding a campaign sets a **rate per 1,000 views**, an optional flat fee per approved submission, a **minimum payout** per video (which decides what reaches the review queue), and a **maximum payout** per video. Whop pays automatically once a submission is approved. Whop's Content Rewards terms set a **10% Content Reward Fee**, deducted from the seller's account on amounts paid to clippers.

- **Suits:** clippers with posting accounts who want the widest choice of campaigns
- **Pros:** many campaigns across niches, caps and rules shown up front
- **Cons:** submissions can be rejected against the brief, popular budgets run out, income swings with views

### Vyro

**How it pays:** CPM. Vyro was unveiled by MrBeast in October 2025. Per Vyro's site as of 2026, each campaign lists a rate per 1,000 views (sample listings showed $1,500 and $2,500 per million views, or **$1.50 and $2.50 per 1,000**). You post to Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, or X and submit the link. Posts must reach a minimum view threshold, earnings stay pending until a final view check after the campaign ends, and you can withdraw from **$10, once per week**, through Stripe or PayPal depending on your location.

- **Suits:** clippers who want campaigns from large creators
- **Pros:** rates published on each listing, big-name campaigns (MrBeast and Mark Rober were among the first), low withdrawal minimum
- **Cons:** money waits for the campaign to end, budgets are finite, and its how-it-works page does not spell out a clipper fee, so read the current terms

### ClipFarm

**How it pays:** CPM. ClipFarm is the clipping community YouTuber Airrack (Eric Decker) launched with Whop, announced on Whop's blog in August 2025. Campaigns run through Whop's Content Rewards. Of the 48 campaigns shown on its public listing in October 2026, rates ran from **$0.05 to $3 per 1,000 views**, most paid $1 to $1.50, and the lowest rates were on music-promotion and watermark campaigns.

- **Suits:** clippers who want campaigns plus a large community
- **Pros:** branded campaigns, a big Discord and Whop community, payouts handled by Whop
- **Cons:** the same volatility as any CPM campaign, no published standard rate, low-rate campaigns mixed in with good ones

More detail in [ClipFarm alternatives](/alternatives/clipfarm-alternatives).

### ClipAffiliates

**How it pays:** CPM, plus UGC campaigns where you make original content. Per its site as of 2026, brands set their own CPM and budget, clippers post to their own accounts and earn per verified view, and brands get a **72-hour window** to approve or reject each clip. It states a **9% fee on brand deposits** and a **9% fee on clipper payouts**.

- **Suits:** clippers adding a second campaign source, or who also want UGC work
- **Pros:** fees stated openly, UGC as well as clipping, no follower minimum
- **Cons:** earnings depend on performance, and a third-party review reports a 9% fee on clipper payouts (check its current pricing)

### Discord campaign servers

**How it pays:** CPM or flat bounties. Streamers and clipping groups post briefs in their own Discord and pay under their own rules.

- **Suits:** clippers already inside a streamer's community
- **Pros:** close to the creator, niche-specific, sometimes a path to paid client work
- **Cons:** tracking, payout timing, and reliability vary widely, so ask how views are verified and when you get paid before you start

### Clipping agencies and managed networks

**How they pay:** mostly per view, at a rate the agency sets. Three examples with public figures:

- **Clipping**, the startup profiled by Bloomberg in 2025, paid editors **$300 to $1,500 per million views**, as reported by Complex in November 2025.
- **Clipping Culture**, per a September 2025 Net Influencer profile, paid about $0.10 per 1,000 views for simple sound-use posts and $2 to $3 for complex work.
- **Lumina Clippers** describes itself on its site as a clipping agency for brands that pays clippers per 1,000 verified views.

In short:

- **Suits:** clippers who want a steady flow of briefs without hunting for campaigns
- **Pros:** vetted rosters, ready-made briefs
- **Cons:** you are one of many clippers, and the agency sets your rate

### Fiverr and Upwork

**How they pay:** fixed. As of 2026, Fiverr keeps **20%** of each order, and Upwork charges freelancers a variable service fee of **0% to 15%** per contract. Check each platform's current fee pages.

- **Suits:** editors who also take non-clipping work
- **Pros:** large buyer pools, platform-handled payments
- **Cons:** you compete with every kind of video editor, and fees cut into small clip orders

See [Kreatli vs. Fiverr](/compare/kreatli-vs-fiverr) for the head-to-head.

### Kreatli Marketplace

**How it pays:** fixed. Creators post clipping jobs and set the pay when they post: per project, hourly, monthly, or yearly. Clippers sign up free, build a profile with samples, and apply. You deliver finished clips to the creator, who posts them on their own channels. Kreatli does not process payments or hold escrow, so payment is arranged directly between creator and clipper.

- **Suits:** clippers without big posting accounts, and anyone who wants repeat clients
- **Pros:** paid for the edit, no posting accounts needed, retainers and ongoing work
- **Cons:** no viral upside, you have to win jobs, a narrower clipping-only job pool than general freelance sites, and no platform payment protection, so agree terms in writing

## What did not make the list

- **OpenClip** is clip-production software for repurposing your own videos. It does not pay clippers.
- **FindClout** mainly sells pay-per-view distribution to brands through networks of account operators. It is closer to a page network than an open clipping marketplace.
- **AI tools** like OpusClip speed up first cuts but do not pay you.

## How to choose

1. **No accounts with views yet?** Start with fixed pay. You earn from your first job and build a portfolio.
2. **Accounts that reliably get views?** CPM campaigns can pay well. Compare caps and thresholds, not just the headline rate.
3. **Need a predictable month?** Combine one or two fixed-pay clients with a few CPM campaigns for upside.

## FAQ

### What is the best clipping platform for beginners?

Without an audience on your posting accounts, fixed pay usually earns faster, because you are paid for the edit. If your accounts already get steady views, Whop Content Rewards and Vyro are common CPM starting points.

### Which clipping platform pays the most?

No single platform pays the most. CPM earnings depend on the campaign rate, caps, and how your clips perform, while fixed pay depends on the rate you agree. Public CPM rates in 2026 mostly sit between about $0.50 and $3 per 1,000 views.

### Do clipping platforms charge clippers fees?

Some do. As of 2026, ClipAffiliates lists a 9% fee on brand deposits and a third-party review reports one on clipper payouts, Whop's terms deduct a 10% fee from the seller, Fiverr keeps 20% of each order, and Upwork charges 0% to 15%. Kreatli is free to join.

### Can I use more than one clipping platform?

Yes. Many clippers run CPM campaigns on two platforms and keep one or two fixed-pay clients. Follow each campaign's rules, and keep client clips off your own accounts unless the client agrees.

### Is Kreatli Marketplace a CPM platform?

No. Kreatli does not run CPM or per-view campaigns. Creators post jobs with fixed pay per project, per hour, per month, or per year, and payment is arranged directly between creator and clipper.
