# How Much Do Clippers Make? Per Clip, Per 1,000 Views, and Per Month

**How much do clippers make?** It depends on which model you work in. Freelance clippers paid per clip typically earn **$15–$35 for a simple talking-head clip** and **$50–$75+ for complex gaming or motion work**, while clippers in CPM campaigns earn per 1,000 views, with campaigns commonly advertising roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**. Monthly income ranges from pocket money to a full-time wage depending on volume, skill, and which model you lean on.

This guide breaks down both income types with worked arithmetic so you can estimate your own numbers. To plug in your own rates, use the [clipping earnings calculator](/tools/clipping-earnings-calculator).

## The two ways clippers get paid

Clippers earn money in two very different ways, and most confusion about "clipper income" comes from mixing them up.

### Fixed pay: per clip, per project, or retainer

In the freelance model, a creator hires you to edit clips and you deliver finished files to them. The creator posts on their own channels. You get paid for the **work**, not for how the clip performs. Common structures:

- **Per clip:** a set price for each finished short
- **Per project or batch:** a price for a defined package, such as 15 clips from one podcast episode
- **Retainer:** a monthly fee for an agreed volume, such as 20 clips per week

This is how [clipping jobs on Kreatli Marketplace](/clipping-jobs) work. Creators post jobs with hourly, per-project, monthly, or yearly pay, and you agree the rate before you start.

### CPM pay: per 1,000 views

In CPM or "content rewards" campaigns, you edit clips from a creator's or brand's footage, **post them on your own accounts**, and earn based on views. A campaign sets a rate per 1,000 views, often with a **minimum view threshold** before a clip qualifies and a **payout cap** per clip. See [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping) for the basics.

CPM has real upside. A clip that takes off can pay more than a week of freelance work. It also carries real volatility, since a well-made clip that stalls at a few hundred views earns nothing.

## Worked examples: fixed pay

Fixed pay is simple multiplication, which is exactly why many clippers like it.

**Example 1: Per-clip freelancer**

- 20 clips per week × $25 per clip = **$500 per week**
- $500 × 4 weeks = **$2,000 per month**

**Example 2: Retainer client**

- A podcast pays a $1,200 monthly retainer for 12 clips per week (48 clips per month)
- $1,200 ÷ 48 = **$25 per clip effective rate**

**Example 3: Higher-skill gaming work**

- 8 gaming highlight clips per week × $60 = **$480 per week**
- $480 × 4 = **$1,920 per month**, from fewer clips

The key metric in fixed pay is your **effective hourly rate**. If a $25 clip takes you 30 minutes including review and export, you earn $50 per hour. If it takes 90 minutes because the client sends unmarked four-hour VODs, you earn about $17 per hour. Price the source review, not just the edit.

## Worked examples: CPM pay

CPM math has more moving parts. The basic formula is:

**Earnings = (views ÷ 1,000) × rate per 1,000 views**, subject to minimums and caps.

**Example 4: A batch of 10 posted clips**

- 10 clips posted, averaging 15,000 views each = 150,000 total views
- 150,000 ÷ 1,000 × $1 = **$150**

**Example 5: Minimum thresholds matter**

Averages hide the distribution. Suppose a campaign requires 2,000 views before a clip qualifies:

- 6 clips get under 2,000 views → **$0**
- 3 clips get 8,000 views each → 24,000 views × $1 / 1,000 = **$24**
- 1 clip gets 120,000 views → **$120**
- Total: **$144** for 10 clips

**Example 6: Caps limit the upside**

A clip hits 2,000,000 views at $1.50 per 1,000 views. Uncapped, that is $3,000. If the campaign caps payouts at $500 per clip, you earn **$500**. Always read the cap before you assume a viral clip pays in full. The [payout cap glossary entry](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning) covers how these limits work.

Also check whether the platform takes a fee from payouts and whether views must be verified before you are paid. Those details vary by platform, so check their current terms.

## What drives clipper rates

Whether you work per clip or per view, a few factors push your earnings up or down.

### For fixed-pay clippers

- **Complexity:** captions-only talking-head clips sit at the low end; gaming SFX, motion graphics, and memes sit higher
- **Source review:** finding moments in a raw four-hour stream is worth more than editing pre-timestamped segments
- **Niche knowledge:** clippers who understand a specific game, finance, or podcast genre can charge more
- **Turnaround:** same-day or 24-hour delivery commands a premium
- **Portfolio and reviews:** proof of past results makes higher rates easier to justify

### For CPM clippers

- **Account reach:** an established account with followers gets more initial distribution
- **Hook quality:** the first few seconds decide whether a clip travels
- **Campaign rate and cap:** a $3 rate with a low cap may pay less than a $1 rate with a high cap
- **Volume:** CPM income usually depends on posting many clips and letting a few winners carry the total
- **Competition:** popular campaigns attract many clippers posting similar moments

These are **illustrative examples**, not data about what clippers actually earn. They show how the numbers combine.

| Beginner | 1 client, 10 clips/week × $15 = $600/mo | 30 clips posted, mostly under thresholds, ~$50 | ~$650 |

## How to raise what you earn

- **Specialize.** A clipper known for podcast clips or a specific game wins repeat work faster than a generalist. See [how to become a clipper](/guides/how-to-become-a-clipper) for building that positioning.
- **Sell batches and retainers.** Recurring work removes the time you spend hunting for the next job.
- **Track your time per clip.** Raise prices on work that takes longer than you quoted for.
- **Build a portfolio that shows results.** Before-and-after edits and niche samples help you charge more.
- **Treat CPM as upside.** Post your best moments, but do not rely on view pay for rent.

## FAQ

### How much do clippers make per clip?

Freelance clippers typically charge **$15–$35 per clip** for simple talking-head or podcast edits and **$50–$75+** for complex gaming, motion graphics, or heavy sound design work. Rates rise with niche expertise, faster turnaround, and when you must review raw footage to find the moments yourself.

### How much do clippers make per 1,000 views?

CPM clipping campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**. Your actual take depends on minimum view thresholds, per-clip payout caps, any platform fees, and whether views are verified. A clip that does not reach the minimum typically earns nothing.

### Can you make a full-time income from clipping?

Some clippers do, usually by combining several fixed-pay clients or retainers with selective CPM campaigns. Income at that level depends on volume, speed, and repeat clients. Treat any monthly figure you see online, including the examples in this guide, as illustration rather than a guarantee.

### Is per-clip pay or CPM better for beginners?

Per-clip pay is more predictable because you know the payment before you start. CPM can work for beginners willing to post often and accept that most clips may earn little. Many beginners start with small fixed-pay jobs to build a portfolio and test CPM campaigns on the side.

### How do I calculate my clipping income?

Multiply clips by rate for fixed pay, and use (views ÷ 1,000) × rate for CPM, applying minimums and caps. The [clipping earnings calculator](/tools/clipping-earnings-calculator) does both so you can compare scenarios quickly.
