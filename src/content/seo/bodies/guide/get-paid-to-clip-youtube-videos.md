# How to Get Paid to Clip YouTube Videos

YouTube clipper jobs pay you to turn a creator's long-form videos, podcasts, or livestreams into Shorts, TikToks, and Reels. Most of this work is fixed-pay freelance: a YouTuber sends new uploads, you return a batch of vertical clips, and they post them on their own channels. Some creators also run view-based clipping campaigns. This guide covers both, plus how to find YouTubers who need clippers and what the work typically pays.

## Why YouTubers hire clippers

Long-form creators know Shorts can bring new viewers to their main channel, but cutting clips from every upload takes hours they would rather spend on the next video. A good clipper solves three problems at once:

- **Volume.** One 30-minute video can produce 5 to 15 usable clips.
- **Consistency.** Clips go out on a schedule instead of whenever the creator finds time.
- **Cross-posting.** The same clips can go to TikTok and Instagram Reels with minor tweaks.

Podcasters are especially strong clients. A weekly two-hour episode is full of quotable moments, and most hosts do not want to edit them.

## Types of YouTube clipping work

### Long-form to Shorts

The core job. You take an uploaded video, find self-contained moments, and cut them to 15 to 60 seconds in 9:16. For talking-head content, that means tight jump cuts, captions, and a hook. For commentary or educational channels, you may add on-screen text, simple zooms, or B-roll.

### Podcast clipping

Video podcasts usually have two or more speakers on fixed cameras. Your job is to find standalone stories, opinions, and exchanges, frame the active speaker, and caption cleanly. See the [podcast clipping definition](/glossary/podcast-clipping-meaning) for how this niche works.

### Livestream and premiere highlights

Creators who stream on YouTube need the same VOD workflow Twitch streamers do: review the broadcast, log moments, and cut highlights fast.

### Campaign clipping

Some YouTubers and brands fund campaigns where clippers post clips on their own accounts and earn per 1,000 views. Commonly advertised rates are roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**, with view minimums and payout caps. This can pay well if you are good at distribution, but income is unpredictable.

## How to find YouTubers who need a clipper

### Look for the gap

The best prospects have a strong long-form channel but a weak or empty Shorts tab. Signs to look for:

- Uploads weekly or more, with healthy view counts on long-form
- Few Shorts, or Shorts that are just unedited cuts
- A podcast or interview format with lots of quotable moments
- An active community that already shares timestamps in comments

### Where to search

- **Kreatli Marketplace.** Creators post [clipping jobs](/clipping-jobs) with deliverables, pay type, and schedule listed up front. Apply with a cover letter and relevant samples.
- **YouTube itself.** Search your niche, sort by recent uploads, and note channels that fit the gap above.
- **Creator Discords and communities.** Many YouTubers post editor openings to their own communities first.
- **Direct outreach.** Use the business email on the channel's About page.

### Pitch with proof

Your pitch should include **one clip cut from their own recent video**, a simple offer, and a turnaround time. For example: "I cut this from your latest episode. I can deliver 8 Shorts within 48 hours of each upload for a fixed weekly price." Keep it short and do not post the sample publicly without their permission.

## What YouTube clippers get paid

Fixed-pay ranges for freelance clip work typically look like this:

| Clip type | Typical pay |
| --- | --- |
| Simple talking-head or podcast clip | $15–$35 per clip |
| Commentary or education with text and B-roll | Middle of the range or higher |
| Gaming, heavy motion graphics, or sound design | $50–$75+ per clip |
| Monthly retainer | A set number of clips per week for a fixed monthly price |

Retainers are the goal. They make your income predictable and let you build templates around one creator's style. For a deeper breakdown, see [how much clippers make](/guides/how-much-do-clippers-make).

## A YouTube clipping workflow

1. **Get source files, not just links.** Ask for the original export or a high-quality download so your clips stay sharp.
2. **Use the transcript.** Skim the auto-transcript to find strong lines faster than watching in real time.
3. **Check comments.** Viewers often timestamp the best moments for you.
4. **Cut for a cold viewer.** Each Short must make sense to someone who never saw the original video.
5. **Hook in the first two seconds.** Lead with the claim, the question, or the punchline. See [why the first three seconds matter](/glossary/video-hook-first-3-seconds).
6. **Caption and frame.** Burned-in captions, faces centered, nothing important hidden behind the platform's on-screen buttons.
7. **Deliver with titles.** Suggested titles or on-screen text options save the creator time and make you harder to replace.

## Skills that make YouTube clippers stand out

- **Story sense.** Pulling a 45-second arc out of a 10-minute tangent is the core skill.
- **Brand consistency.** Matching fonts, colors, and caption style across every Short.
- **Platform awareness.** Small adjustments for TikTok and Reels when the creator cross-posts.
- **Reliability.** Creators keep clippers who deliver on time every week, even over more flashy editors.

## Permission

When a YouTuber hires you, they are authorizing you to edit their content, and they post the results. Clipping someone's videos and posting them on your own channel without permission is a different situation, and can lead to copyright claims or strikes. Read [do you need permission to clip streams](/guides/do-you-need-permission-to-clip-streams) for general information and a permission template.

## FAQ

### How do I get YouTube clipper jobs?

Build 5 to 10 vertical samples in one niche, then apply to clipping jobs on marketplaces like Kreatli and pitch creators directly with a sample cut from their own content. Target channels with strong long-form views but few Shorts.

### How much do YouTube clippers get paid?

Freelance YouTube clips typically pay $15–$35 for simple talking-head or podcast clips and $50–$75+ for complex gaming or motion work. Many clippers move clients to monthly retainers once the relationship is stable.

### Can I clip YouTube videos and post them on my own channel?

Only with permission or as part of an official campaign. Reposting someone else's content without permission can lead to copyright claims, strikes, or demonetization. Fixed-pay clipping avoids this because the creator posts the clips.

### How many Shorts can you get from one YouTube video?

It depends on the format. A 20-minute talking-head video might give 3 to 8 strong clips, while a two-hour podcast can give 10 or more. Quality matters more than count, so cut only moments that stand on their own.
