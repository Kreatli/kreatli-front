# Is Clipping a Good Side Hustle? Time, Pay and Who It Suits

**Clipping can be a good side hustle if you can edit, can commit a few hours a week, and want small, steady income.** It's a poor fit if you expect fast or passive money. There are two ways to do it on the side: fixed-pay client work, where a creator pays you per clip and posts the clips themselves, and view-based campaigns, where you post clips on your own accounts and get paid per 1,000 views. The first is steadier. The second can pay more on a good week but often pays nothing.

## The two ways to clip on the side

### Fixed-pay client work

A creator, such as a streamer, podcaster or YouTuber, pays you to turn their long videos into short clips. You agree a price per clip, per batch or per month. You send the files, and they post them on their own channels.

- **You don't need followers.** The clips go on the creator's channels.
- **Pay is known upfront.** Simple clips typically pay $15 to $35 each. See [clipper pay rates](/guides/clipper-pay-rates).
- **You need a client.** Finding the first one takes the most effort.

This is how clipping works on Kreatli Marketplace. Creators post jobs with the pay already set, and you apply. You agree terms directly with the creator, because Kreatli doesn't process payments.

### View-based campaigns (CPM)

A creator or brand funds a campaign. You download approved footage, post clips on your own TikTok, Shorts or Reels accounts, and earn based on views. Campaigns commonly advertise roughly $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views, usually with a minimum view count and a cap per clip. See [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping).

- **You can start without a client.** Anyone can join most campaigns.
- **Pay is unpredictable.** A clip that misses the minimum earns nothing.
- **Reach matters.** Accounts with an audience tend to do better.

Many clippers do both: client work for a steady base, and campaigns for upside.

## How much time it takes

There's no official figure for how long a clip takes. It depends on your speed, the footage and the style. Here's a worked example with clear assumptions:

- You're editing simple podcast clips with captions.
- Each clip takes about **30 minutes**, including finding the moment, editing and exporting.
- You have **5 hours a week** on weeknights.

That's about **10 clips a week**. If the client sends long, unmarked streams, finding the moments takes much longer, and you might manage half that.

Expect your first clips to take longer. Speed comes with practice and good templates.

## What it pays for the hours

These examples use the figures in [how much clippers make](/guides/how-much-do-clippers-make) and the pay guide. They're examples, not promises.

**Fixed pay, steady client:**

- 10 simple clips a week × $25 = **$250 a week**, or about **$1,000 a month**
- At 30 minutes a clip, that's about **$50 an hour**
- If clips take 90 minutes each, the same $25 clip works out to about **$17 an hour**

**Starting out:**

- 10 clips a week at $15 = **$150 a week**, or about **$600 a month**
- That's the beginner example from our earnings guide, and a common place to start

**Campaigns:**

- 10 clips posted at $1 per 1,000 views, with a 2,000-view minimum
- 6 miss the minimum and earn **$0**. 3 get 8,000 views each and earn **$24** together. 1 gets 120,000 views and earns **$120**.
- Total: **$144** for 10 clips, which could be most of your week's work, or close to nothing if none take off

Retainers with steady creators typically run about $800 to $3,500 or more a month, but that's usually more than a few hours a week.

You'll see claims online of $5,000 to $20,000 a month from clipping. Those are outliers, often from people selling a course or a platform. Don't plan around them.

## Who clipping suits (and who it doesn't)

**It suits you if you:**

- Already edit a bit, or are willing to learn CapCut or Premiere Pro
- Watch a lot of streams, podcasts or YouTube and can spot a good moment
- Can stick to a weekly schedule
- Are fine with a few hundred dollars a month while you build up clients

**It's a poor fit if you:**

- Want passive income. Every clip is active work.
- Can't commit regular hours. Clients want clips on a schedule.
- Need money fast. Building a portfolio and finding clients takes weeks.

## Costs and risks

- **Software:** CapCut is free to start. Premiere Pro or other paid tools add a monthly cost.
- **Unpaid work on campaigns:** clips that miss the view minimum, or are rejected, earn nothing.
- **Scams:** be careful with anyone who asks you to pay upfront or keeps asking for unpaid tests. See [is clipping legit](/guides/is-clipping-legit).
- **Permission:** only clip content you have permission to use. See [do you need permission to clip streams](/guides/do-you-need-permission-to-clip-streams).

## How to start this week

1. **Pick a niche** you already watch, such as gaming, podcasts or fitness.
2. **Set up CapCut** and learn cutting, 9:16 reframing and captions.
3. **Make 5 sample clips** from your own content or content you have permission to use.
4. **Put them in a simple portfolio**, like a Google Drive folder or a profile page.
5. **Apply to clipping jobs** on the [clipping jobs page](/clipping-jobs), or message small creators whose Shorts tab is empty.
6. **Track your time** on each clip so you know your real hourly rate.

## FAQ

### Is clipping worth it in 2026?

It can be, if you treat it as real part-time work. Fixed-pay clients typically pay $15 to $35 per simple clip, which can work out to a fair hourly rate once you're fast. It's not worth it if you expect passive or fast money.

### How much can you make clipping on the side?

In our examples, a beginner doing 10 simple clips a week at $15 earns about $600 a month, and 10 clips a week at $25 is about $1,000 a month. Campaign income varies a lot and can be close to zero. Your result depends on your speed, rates and clients.

### Do you need followers to start clipping?

Not for client work. With fixed-pay clipping, the creator posts the clips on their own channels, so your follower count doesn't matter. For view-based campaigns, you post on your own accounts, so an audience helps a lot.

### How many hours a week does clipping take?

As much as you choose to take on. As a rough example, a simple clip might take about 30 minutes once you're practiced, so 5 hours a week is about 10 clips. Long unmarked streams take much longer.

### How do you get your first clipping clients?

Build a small portfolio of 5 to 10 clips, then apply to clipping jobs and message small creators who post long videos but few Shorts. Send one sample clip cut from their own video, with a clear price and turnaround.

### Is clipping passive income?

No. Every clip takes active work: finding the moment, editing and exporting. Campaign income can arrive after you've posted, but you still have to make and post each clip first.
