# Clipper Pay Rates in 2026: Per Clip, Per Hour and Per 1,000 Views

Typical **clipper pay rates** in 2026 are **$15 to $35 per simple clip**, **$50 to $75 or more per complex clip**, monthly retainers of roughly **$800 to $3,500 or more**, and CPM campaign rates of about **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**. Where you land depends on edit complexity, source footage, turnaround, and, for CPM work, how your clips perform.

These are **market ranges** drawn from public rates and freelance norms, not Kreatli platform data. On Kreatli Marketplace, each creator sets the pay when they post a job. For worked income math, see [how much clippers make](/guides/how-much-do-clippers-make). For budgeting as a creator, see [how much to pay clippers](/guides/how-much-to-pay-clippers).

## Per-clip rates by complexity

Per-clip pricing is the most common fixed-pay structure. The rate follows the work involved:

- **$15 to $35: simple clips.** Talking-head or podcast moments with captions, from timestamps the creator provides.
- **$30 to $50: mid-level clips.** B-roll, punch-ins, branded caption styles, light sound design.
- **$50 to $75+: complex clips.** Gaming highlights with SFX and memes, heavier sound design.
- **$75+: motion-heavy clips.** Custom motion graphics or heavily designed edits.

Two things push any clip up a band: **finding the moments yourself** in a raw multi-hour VOD, and **fast turnaround**. A clip cut from three marked timestamps is a different job from one pulled out of a four-hour stream with no notes.

## Hourly rates

Hourly pay is less common for pure clipping, because creators find it harder to budget, but it suits open-ended work like reviewing a back catalog or building caption templates. Creators on Kreatli can post hourly jobs alongside per-project, monthly, and yearly ones.

The useful number is your **effective hourly rate**: what you earn per clip divided by the time it takes, including source review and export.

| Rate per clip | Time per clip | Effective hourly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $25 | 30 minutes | $50 |
| $25 | 90 minutes | About $17 |
| $60 | 60 minutes | $60 |
| $15 | 20 minutes | $45 |

If you quote hourly, set it so a typical clip lands in the per-clip bands above. If you price per clip, track your time so slow jobs do not quietly drop your rate.

## Monthly retainer ranges

Once a creator needs clips every week, a retainer is usually simpler for both sides. Freelance clip retainers typically run about **$800 to $3,500 or more per month**, depending on volume, niche, and complexity.

Retainers usually carry a slightly lower effective per-clip rate in exchange for steady volume. Two examples:

- 12 podcast clips per week (48 per month) for $1,200 works out to **$25 per clip**
- 20 clips per week (80 per month) at $22 per clip comes to **$1,760 per month**

Spell out weekly volume, turnaround, revision rounds, and what happens to unused clips.

## CPM campaign rates

In CPM campaigns you post clips on your own accounts and earn per 1,000 views at a rate the campaign sets. Across platforms, advertised rates commonly sit around **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**. Publicly stated examples:

| Source | Stated rate | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vyro campaign listings (Vyro site, as of 2026) | $1.50 and $2.50 per 1,000 views | Sample campaign listings |
| ClipFarm campaign listing (October 2026) | $0.05 to $3 per 1,000 views; most of the 48 shown paid $1 to $1.50 | Live and recent campaigns |
| Whop Content Rewards terms | "$1 per 1000 views" | Example rate in the terms |
| Clipping, per Bloomberg (reported by Complex, November 2025) | $300 to $1,500 per million views ($0.30 to $1.50 per 1,000) | What the agency paid editors |
| Clipping Culture (Net Influencer, September 2025) | About $0.10 per 1,000 for simple sound-use posts; $2 to $3 for complex work | Agency rates |
| NPR (May 2026) | $0.50 per 1,000 (Polymarket); $1 per 1,000 (MLB clips via Content Rewards); $25 per 1,000 (an AI startup) | Individual campaigns |

The $25 example is an outlier, not a typical rate. In the listings we checked, most campaigns clustered around $1 to $1.50 per 1,000 views.

To turn a rate into earnings: **(views ÷ 1,000) × rate**, then apply the campaign's minimums and caps. The [clipping earnings calculator](/tools/clipping-earnings-calculator) does this for you.

## Caps, thresholds and fees

The headline CPM rate is only part of the deal. Check these before you start:

- **Minimum payout or view threshold.** Whop's documentation describes a minimum payout per video that decides when a clip enters review. Its example: at $3 per 1,000 views with a $6 minimum, a clip needs about 2,000 views. Vyro's site says approved posts must reach a minimum view threshold before they earn.
- **Maximum payout per clip.** Whop's example: a $3,000 cap at $3 per 1,000 views stops paying at 1 million views. See [payout caps](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning).
- **Campaign budget.** Vyro pays until the campaign's budget runs out. A strong clip submitted late can earn little.
- **Approval.** Most platforms review clips against the brief, and a rejected clip earns nothing.
- **Fees.** As of 2026, ClipAffiliates lists a 9% fee on brand deposits (a third-party review also reports a fee on clipper payouts, so check its current pricing), and Whop's Content Rewards terms deduct a 10% fee from the seller. Vyro sets a $10 minimum withdrawal, once per week.

A $3 rate with a low cap can pay less than a $1 rate with a high cap. Compare the whole package.

## What moves clipper pay rates

**For fixed-pay clips:**

- **Complexity:** captions-only edits sit low, gaming SFX and motion graphics sit high
- **Source review:** raw VODs cost more than timestamped segments
- **Niche knowledge:** gaming, finance, and technical topics reward clippers who know the content
- **Turnaround:** 24-hour delivery commands a premium
- **Volume:** guaranteed weekly volume justifies a lower per-clip rate
- **Portfolio:** samples with results make higher rates easier to justify

**For CPM campaigns:**

- **Account reach:** established accounts get more initial distribution
- **Hook quality:** the first seconds decide whether a clip travels
- **Cap and threshold:** these often matter more than the headline rate
- **Competition:** popular campaigns attract many clippers posting similar moments

## FAQ

### What is a good per-clip rate for a clipper?

For simple talking-head or podcast clips, $15 to $35 is typical. Complex gaming or motion work runs $50 to $75 or more. Charge toward the top when you review raw footage, deliver fast, or bring niche expertise.

### How much do clipping campaigns pay per 1,000 views?

Most advertised rates in 2026 sit around $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views, with many campaigns near $1. Your real earnings depend on minimum thresholds, payout caps, approval, fees, and whether the budget lasts.

### What is a typical monthly retainer for a clipper?

Roughly $800 to $3,500 or more per month, depending on how many clips per week, how complex they are, and the turnaround. Retainers often carry a slightly lower per-clip rate in exchange for guaranteed volume.

### Are these Kreatli's rates?

No. These are market ranges from public rates and freelance norms. On Kreatli Marketplace, each creator sets the pay (per project, hourly, monthly, or yearly) when posting a job, and payment is arranged directly between creator and clipper.

### Is per-clip pay or CPM better?

Per-clip pay is predictable and pays even when a clip flops. CPM can pay more on a clip that takes off but often pays little on the rest. Many clippers combine a fixed-pay client with a few campaigns.
