# How to Get Paid to Clip Twitch Streams

You can get paid to clip streams on Twitch in two main ways: a streamer hires you to turn their VODs into Shorts, TikToks, and Reels for fixed pay, or you join a clipping campaign that pays per 1,000 views on clips you post yourself. Either way, the work is the same at its core: find the moments that make sense to someone who was not watching live, cut them tight, caption them, and deliver fast.

This guide covers how to find streamers who need clippers, a VOD workflow that does not waste hours, when to use Twitch's clip tool versus a full VOD edit, and how pay works.

## Who pays Twitch clippers

### Streamers who want growth off Twitch

Twitch discovery is hard. Many streamers rely on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Reels to bring new viewers to their channel. Those who stream several times a week rarely have time to edit, so they hire clippers. These are your best fixed-pay clients.

### Clipping campaigns

Some streamers and brands run campaigns where any approved clipper can post clips on their own accounts and earn per view. Pay commonly advertised is roughly **$0.50–$3 per 1,000 views**, usually with **minimum view thresholds** and **payout caps**. Read the [clipping campaign definition](/glossary/what-is-a-clipping-campaign) if you are new to the model.

### Agencies and creator teams

Larger streamers sometimes have managers or small production teams that hire freelance clippers in batches. These roles often come with clearer briefs and steadier volume.

## How to find Twitch streamers who need a clipper

Look for streamers who fit this profile:

- **Stream 3+ times a week** with consistent categories
- **Have a few hundred to a few thousand average viewers**, large enough to pay, small enough to not have a full team
- **Post few or no Shorts**, or post them inconsistently
- **Have an active Discord**, which often has a channel for clips or job posts

Ways to reach them:

- Apply to [clipping jobs on Kreatli Marketplace](/clipping-jobs), where creators list stream content, deliverables, and pay
- Check streamer Discords for "editor wanted" or "clipper applications" posts
- Send a short DM or email with **one sample clip made from their own stream**, plus a simple offer

A good first message: "I cut this from Tuesday's stream. I can deliver 10 vertical clips within 48 hours of each stream for a fixed weekly price. Want a trial batch?"

## Twitch clip tool vs VOD editing

Twitch has a built-in clip tool that lets viewers and the streamer grab short moments during or after a broadcast. It is useful, but it is not the same as editing.

| | Twitch clip tool | Full VOD editing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | Instant | Takes review and edit time |
| Control | Limited trimming | Full control over cuts, captions, framing |
| Format | Twitch's native clip | Custom 9:16 exports for each platform |
| Best for | Marking moments, community clips | Finished Shorts, TikToks, Reels, highlight videos |

The smart workflow uses both. Community and mod clips act as **free timestamps**. You use them to find moments fast, then pull the full-quality footage from the VOD and edit properly.

## A VOD workflow that saves hours

Reviewing a four-hour stream in real time is not sustainable. Use a system.

1. **Get the VOD fast.** Twitch only keeps past broadcasts for a limited time (commonly reported as 14 days for Affiliates and 60 days for Partners and Turbo/Prime users), so download or ask for a local copy soon after each stream.
2. **Collect markers.** Pull community clips, stream markers, chat spikes, and any timestamps the streamer or mods share.
3. **Skim at speed.** Watch at 1.5x to 2x, focusing on the areas around markers and chat spikes.
4. **Log moments.** Keep a simple timestamp list with a one-line note per moment.
5. **Cut the setup.** Start as close to the payoff as possible while keeping enough context to make sense.
6. **Reframe for vertical.** Facecam on top and gameplay below is common for gaming. For Just Chatting, center the streamer.
7. **Caption and polish.** Burned-in captions, sound balancing, and a hook in the first two seconds.
8. **Deliver with a log.** Send finished files plus the timestamp list. Streamers love it, and it builds trust.

Ask early about **music**. DMCA-protected music in a stream can cause takedowns once it is posted elsewhere. Mute or replace it when needed.

## What Twitch clippers get paid

### Fixed-pay freelance

Typical ranges for stream clips:

- **Simple clips from timestamped moments:** around $15–$35 per clip
- **Full VOD review plus gaming edits, SFX, memes, or motion graphics:** $50–$75+ per clip
- **Weekly packages:** a set number of clips per stream or per week, billed weekly or monthly

Full VOD review should cost more than working from timestamps, because finding the moments is part of the work. Price that in.

### CPM campaigns

Your earnings depend on views your own accounts get, minus anything that fails verification. One clip can pay well, while dozens may earn little or nothing. Read the rules on [verified views](/glossary/verified-views-meaning) before you post.

For a fuller picture of both models, see [how much clippers make](/guides/how-much-do-clippers-make).

## Get permission before you post

If a streamer hires you, they are giving you permission to edit their content, and they post the finished clips on their channel. If you want to post clips on your own accounts, you need the streamer's okay or an official campaign. Game footage, music, and other people appearing on stream add more layers. Our guide on [whether you need permission to clip streams](/guides/do-you-need-permission-to-clip-streams) includes a free permission template.

## Stand out as a Twitch clipper

- **Know the culture.** Emotes, inside jokes, and chat context matter. Clips that miss the joke will not get used.
- **Be fast.** Stream moments feel stale quickly. Delivering within 24 to 72 hours is a real advantage.
- **Respect boundaries.** Skip private moments, sponsor-sensitive segments, and anything the streamer asks you to avoid.
- **Offer a long-form option.** Weekly YouTube highlight videos are a natural upsell.

## FAQ

### How do you get paid to clip Twitch streams?

Either a streamer hires you for fixed pay per clip, batch, or month, or you join a clipping campaign that pays per 1,000 views on clips you post yourself. Freelance work is more predictable. Campaigns have more upside and more risk.

### Can I clip any Twitch streamer and post it?

Not safely. Posting another streamer's content without permission can lead to takedowns or account strikes, and you usually cannot monetize it. Get permission or join an official campaign first.

### How much do Twitch clippers make?

Freelance stream clips typically pay $15–$35 for simple work and $50–$75+ for complex gaming edits. Campaign clippers earn per view, so income varies widely from week to week.

### Do I need to watch the whole VOD?

Not usually. Use community clips, stream markers, and chat spikes to locate moments, then skim the rest at higher speed. Ask the streamer or mods for timestamps when possible.

### What software do Twitch clippers use?

Most use CapCut, Premiere Pro, or DaVinci Resolve for editing, plus a tool for downloading VODs and an auto-caption tool for a first pass. Pick one editor and build templates to speed up repeat work.
