# How to Make Money Clipping Streamers: Fixed Pay vs. CPM Campaigns

You make money clipping streamers in two ways. A **streamer pays you directly** per clip, per batch or per month to edit clips for their own channels, or a **clipping campaign pays you per 1,000 views** on clips you post to your own accounts. Clipping a streamer as a fan, with no deal in place, pays nothing. Most clippers start with one model, then add the other once they know what they are good at.

This guide is the overview. It covers how each model pays, how to choose which streamers to clip, the permission question, and how to pitch. For platform details, see the guides for [Twitch](/guides/get-paid-to-clip-twitch-streams), [Kick](/guides/get-paid-to-clip-kick-streams) and [YouTube](/guides/get-paid-to-clip-youtube-videos).

## The two ways clipping streamers pays

| | Hired by the streamer (fixed pay) | Clipping campaign (CPM) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who pays you | The streamer or their team | The campaign budget, through a platform |
| Paid for | The edit | Views on your clips |
| Where clips go | The streamer's own TikTok, Shorts and Reels | Your own accounts |
| Typical pay | $15 to $35 per simple clip, $50 to $75+ for complex edits | About $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views |
| Income | Predictable | Varies with every clip |
| Main risk | Finding and keeping clients | Clips that never reach the view minimum |

The figures come from our [clipper pay rates guide](/guides/clipper-pay-rates). They are market ranges, not promises. Each streamer or campaign sets its own terms.

### Fixed pay: a streamer hires you

The streamer sends you their VODs (the saved recordings of past streams) or timestamps. You return finished vertical clips, and they post them on their own channels. You get paid for the work, whatever the views.

This is the steadier model. A streamer who likes your clips will often move you onto a weekly batch or a monthly retainer. It also builds a portfolio, because your best clips sit on a real channel with real numbers.

### CPM: a campaign pays per view

In a [clipping campaign](/glossary/what-is-a-clipping-campaign), a streamer or brand funds a budget and sets a rate per 1,000 views. You cut clips, post them on your own TikTok, Shorts, Reels or X accounts, and submit the links. Clips that pass review and reach the minimum views earn money, up to a [payout cap](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning).

CPM can pay well when a clip takes off. It can also pay nothing for a week of work, because clips that never reach the minimum earn nothing. Campaigns also only count [verified views](/glossary/verified-views-meaning), which can be lower than what your dashboard shows.

### A worked example

Say you cut 10 clips a week from one streamer.

- **Fixed pay:** 10 clips × $25 = **$250 a week**, or about $1,000 over four weeks.
- **CPM at $1 per 1,000 views:** if 3 of the 10 clips clear the minimum and average 20,000 views, that is 60,000 paid views × $1 ÷ 1,000 = **$60 a week**. If one clip reaches 300,000 views, that clip alone earns $300, unless the cap is lower.

Neither number is a promise. The point is the shape: fixed pay is steady, CPM is lumpy. Run your own numbers with the [clipping earnings calculator](/tools/clipping-earnings-calculator).

## Clipping streamers by platform

Each platform works a little differently. The platform guides go deeper, so this is a quick map.

- **Twitch.** Most streamers already rely on short-form clips to grow, and many with steady schedules hire clippers for fixed pay. Viewers can grab clips with Twitch's built-in tool, but turning a VOD into finished vertical clips is editing work. See [how to get paid to clip Twitch streams](/guides/get-paid-to-clip-twitch-streams).
- **Kick.** Kick has the most visible CPM clipping programs, many run by large streamers. Kick's own monetization programs are for streamers, not clippers, so know exactly who is paying you. See [how to get paid to clip Kick streams](/guides/get-paid-to-clip-kick-streams).
- **YouTube Live.** Streamers on YouTube often want both Shorts and longer highlight videos from the same stream, which suits clippers who can do both. See [how to get paid to clip YouTube videos](/guides/get-paid-to-clip-youtube-videos).

## How to choose the best streamers to clip

Picking the right streamer matters more than editing speed. Look for these signs.

1. **They stream often and on a schedule.** Three or more streams a week means steady footage and steady work.
2. **They are big enough to pay, small enough to need you.** A few hundred to a few thousand average viewers is the usual sweet spot. The largest streamers often have a full team already.
3. **Their streams are clip-able.** Strong reactions, funny moments, clear talking points and a recognizable personality make clips that work for viewers who missed the stream. Long silent stretches of gameplay do not.
4. **Their short-form presence has gaps.** No Shorts, inconsistent posting, or clips that are just raw screen grabs with no captions are all openings.
5. **They already pay clippers, or say they want to.** Look for "clipper wanted" posts in their Discord, a clips channel, or a campaign link in their bio.

Avoid streamers whose content you would not want in your portfolio, and be careful with gambling streams, which some platforms and campaigns restrict.

## Permission and platform rules

Clipping a stream and posting it is using someone else's work. The safest ways to have permission are to be hired by the streamer, to join an official campaign they run, or to get their written permission. Giving credit is not the same as having permission.

Platform rules apply too. Streams often contain music, game footage and other people, each with their own rights. If you plan to run your own clip channel, read about [clip channels](/glossary/clip-channel-meaning) and how platforms treat reused content.

Our guide on [whether you need permission to clip streams](/guides/do-you-need-permission-to-clip-streams) covers this in detail and includes a free permission request template. It is general information, not legal advice.

## How to pitch a streamer

Most fixed-pay work starts with a short message and a sample.

1. **Make one clip from their own stream.** A clip from last night's stream proves more than any portfolio link.
2. **Keep the message short.** Say what you made, what you can deliver, and how fast.
3. **Offer a small paid trial.** For example, 5 clips from one stream at your normal rate.
4. **Name a clear price.** Per clip, per batch or per month, so they can say yes quickly.

A simple pitch: "I cut this from Tuesday's stream. I can deliver 10 vertical clips with captions within 48 hours of each stream for a fixed weekly price. Want a paid trial batch?"

### The portfolio you need

- **3 to 5 vertical clips** in the niche you want, such as gaming, Just Chatting or IRL streams
- **Clips that make sense to a cold viewer**, with the setup kept short and the payoff early
- **Readable captions** placed clear of the app buttons
- **Numbers if you have them**, such as views on clips you posted with permission

You can also browse [clipping jobs](/clipping-jobs) from streamers and creators who have already decided to hire, and skip the cold pitch.

## Staying safe

The boom in "get paid to clip" offers has brought scams with it. Never pay to join a clipping program, be wary of unpaid tests that never end, and read campaign rules on caps, minimums and verification before you start. Our guide to [whether clipping is legit](/guides/is-clipping-legit) has a full checklist.

## FAQ

### Do streamers pay clippers?

Yes. Many streamers pay clippers to turn their streams into short clips for TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Reels. Some hire a clipper directly for fixed pay per clip, batch or month. Others fund campaigns that pay clippers per 1,000 views on clips they post themselves.

### How much do you make clipping streamers?

Fixed-pay clippers typically earn $15 to $35 for a simple clip and $50 to $75 or more for complex edits, and retainers often run about $800 to $3,500 or more a month. CPM campaigns commonly pay about $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views, with minimums and caps. These are market ranges, so your pay depends on the streamer, the work and your speed.

### Do you need permission to clip streamers?

To post a streamer's content and get paid, you should have permission. The usual ways are being hired by the streamer, joining an official campaign they run, or getting written permission. Credit alone is not permission, and music or game footage can add other rights issues.

### Which platform is best for clipping streamers?

It depends on the model you want. Kick has the most visible CPM clipping programs. Twitch and YouTube Live have many streamers who hire clippers for fixed pay. The best choice is the platform whose streamers you understand and whose content you can clip well.

### Is clipping streamers legit?

Yes, clipping streamers is real paid work, both as fixed-pay freelancing and through CPM campaigns. Scams exist, so never pay to join, avoid endless unpaid tests, and read a campaign's payout rules before you start.
