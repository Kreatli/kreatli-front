# Do You Need Permission to Clip Streams? Rights, Copyright, and a Free Permission Template

In most cases, yes: you need permission to clip streams and post them, especially if you plan to post on your own accounts or earn money from the clips. A stream is the streamer's creative work, and it may also contain game footage, music, and other people, each with its own rights. The simplest way to stay safe is to work for the streamer directly, join an official clipping campaign, or get written permission. This page is **general information, not legal advice**. If you have a specific situation, talk to a qualified lawyer in your country.

## Why permission matters

When you clip and repost a stream, you are copying and distributing someone else's content. Without permission, the streamer or other rights holders can:

- File copyright takedown requests against your clips
- Trigger strikes that can restrict or remove your account
- Claim or block monetization on your clips
- Ask platforms to remove your account for repeat issues

Many streamers welcome clips because they bring new viewers. But "they probably don't mind" is not the same as permission, and streamers can change their minds or start official programs that exclude unofficial clippers.

If you want to clip a streamer independently, ask. A short written agreement, even a DM or email, protects both sides. A template is below.

## Copyright basics for clippers

- **Copyright usually belongs to the creator of the content.** For a stream, that is generally the streamer, though their platform's terms may grant the platform certain licenses.
- **Clipping a moment does not make it yours.** Editing, captions, and reframing are your work, but the underlying footage still belongs to its owner.
- **Credit is not permission.** Tagging the streamer or writing "all rights go to the owner" does not give you the right to post.
- **Monetizing makes it riskier.** Earning money from someone else's content without permission makes disputes more likely.

## Platform policies (check current terms)

Each platform has its own rules, and they change. As of 2026, keep these general points in mind and read each platform's current terms:

- **Streaming platforms** such as Twitch, Kick, and YouTube have terms covering who owns uploaded content and how their built-in clip tools can be used. A clip created with a platform's tool lives on that platform. That does not automatically give you the right to download it and repost it elsewhere.
- **Posting platforms** such as TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels have their own copyright, reused-content, and originality rules. Clips that are straight reuploads with little added value may be limited or demonetized even when you have permission.
- **Automated systems** like YouTube's Content ID can claim clips that contain matched audio or video, regardless of whether you meant to infringe.

## Game footage

Video games are copyrighted too. Many game publishers publish **video or content usage policies** that allow streaming and gameplay videos, sometimes with conditions on monetization or specific content such as cutscenes or soundtracks. Policies differ by publisher and by game, so check the relevant one if a clip is mostly gameplay.

## Music is the biggest risk

Music causes more clip takedowns than almost anything else.

- Music playing in the background of a stream is usually owned by a label or publisher, not the streamer.
- Even if the streamer had a license to play it live, that license usually does not cover your clip on another platform.
- Posting platforms may detect the track automatically and mute, block, or claim your clip.

The safe approach is to **mute or cut copyrighted music** and replace it with properly licensed tracks. See our guide to [royalty-free music for clips](/glossary/royalty-free-music-for-clips).

## What about fair use?

**Fair use** is a US legal doctrine that allows limited use of copyrighted material in some situations, such as commentary, criticism, or news. Courts look at several factors case by case, including how transformative the use is and its effect on the market for the original. Other countries have different rules, such as fair dealing, and some are narrower.

For clippers, the key point is that **reposting the best moments of a stream with captions is unlikely to be treated as transformative** by most measures, because it often serves the same purpose as the original. Do not rely on fair use as your plan. Get permission instead.

## Other people on stream

Streams often include guests, co-streamers, and members of the public, especially in IRL content. Even with the streamer's permission, consider:

- Whether guests or co-streamers are okay with being clipped
- Privacy concerns for bystanders, particularly in public IRL streams
- Removing private information that appears on screen, such as addresses or messages

## Free permission request template

Copy this into a DM or email and adjust the details. Keep a record of the reply. You can also [download the permission request template](/templates/clip-permission-request-template.txt).

> Hi [Streamer name],
>
> I'm [Your name], a short-form clip editor. I'd like to clip moments from your streams and post them as vertical clips. Here's what I'm proposing:
>
> - **Content:** clips from your [Twitch / Kick / YouTube] streams and VODs
> - **Where they'll be posted:** [your channel / my accounts: list handles]
> - **Credit:** your name and channel link in every caption
> - **Music:** I'll mute or replace any copyrighted music
> - **Off-limits:** I'll skip anything you tell me to avoid, plus private or sponsor-sensitive moments
> - **Monetization:** [I won't monetize these clips / revenue terms we agree]
> - **Removal:** I'll take down any clip within 48 hours if you ask
> - **Duration:** this permission lasts until either of us ends it in writing
>
> Could you reply "I agree" if this works for you? Here's a sample clip from your recent stream: [link]
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

## FAQ

### Is it illegal to clip streams without permission?

Copying and reposting someone's copyrighted stream without permission can infringe their copyright, which may lead to takedowns, strikes, or legal claims depending on the situation and country. This is general information, not legal advice. Getting permission is the safest approach.

### Can I post Twitch clips on TikTok?

Only with permission from the streamer or as part of an official campaign, and only if the clip follows TikTok's rules. A clip created on Twitch does not automatically give you rights to post it on other platforms.

### Is clipping covered by fair use?

Usually not in a way you can rely on. Fair use is decided case by case, and reposting highlights with captions is often not considered transformative. Get permission rather than counting on fair use.

### Do I need permission if I give credit?

Yes. Credit is good practice but it is not permission. You still need the creator's agreement to post their content.

### What happens if I clip copyrighted music?

The posting platform may mute, block, or claim your clip, and repeated issues can lead to account strikes. Mute or replace copyrighted music with licensed tracks before posting.
