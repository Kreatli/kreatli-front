# How to Brief a Clipper or Video Editor (Free Brief Template)

A good clipper can only do so much with a vague brief. If you want clips that match your voice, platform, and audience, give your clipper or editor enough context to make smart choices. This guide walks through what to include and ends with a free **clipping brief template** you can copy, fill in, and reuse for every batch. **Kreatli Marketplace** helps creators hire clippers, but the best results come from clear briefs before the first batch starts.

The system works for Shorts, TikToks, Reels, podcasts, livestreams, and other creator content. If you have not hired anyone yet, start with [how to hire a clipper](/guides/how-to-hire-a-clipper), then come back here once you have a shortlist.

## What a clipping brief should do

A brief is not a long brand document. It is a working instruction sheet that helps the clipper answer:

- What is the goal of the clip?
- Who is the audience?
- What source footage should be used?
- What style should the edit follow?
- What deliverables are required?
- What should the editor avoid?
- How will feedback work?

The goal is to reduce guessing. Every unanswered question becomes either a delay or a revision.

## The one-page brief structure

Use this structure for most clipping jobs:

1. **Project goal** - Why are you making these clips?
2. **Audience** - Who should care?
3. **Source files** - Where is the footage and what matters?
4. **Deliverables** - How many clips, how long, what format?
5. **Style references** - What should it feel like?
6. **Rules** - Captions, music, words to avoid, brand assets.
7. **Review process** - Deadline, revision rounds, feedback format.

You can write this in a doc, Notion page, spreadsheet, or job post. What matters is clarity.

## Define the goal

"Make this engaging" is not enough. Tell the editor what the clip is supposed to achieve.

Examples:

- Get viewers to watch the full podcast
- Grow YouTube Shorts reach
- Turn webinar answers into LinkedIn clips
- Make a coach's advice feel clear and credible
- Create funny stream highlights for TikTok
- Test hooks for a product launch

Different goals create different edits. A retention clip and a sales clip need different endings. If the clips are part of a larger push across many accounts, see [how to run a clipping campaign](/guides/how-to-run-a-clipping-campaign) for the extra rules campaigns need.

## Share useful source context

Do not drop a folder of files with no explanation. Include:

- Episode or stream title
- Best timestamps
- Sections to avoid
- Sponsor reads or private comments to remove
- Names and roles of speakers
- Correct pronunciation for names or brands
- Transcript, if available

For podcasts and livestreams, timestamps are the biggest time saver. Even rough notes help.

## Give style references

Send three examples:

1. One clip that matches the pacing you want.
2. One clip with caption style you like.
3. One clip that shows what to avoid.

Then explain why. Clippers cannot read your taste from links alone.

Good notes:

- "Use captions like this: large, white, high contrast."
- "I like the first two seconds because it opens on a strong claim."
- "Avoid this many sound effects; it feels too chaotic for my brand."
- "Keep the edit cleaner, like a founder interview, not a meme page."

## Specify deliverables

Write deliverables like a checklist:

- 8 clips
- 30-60 seconds each
- 9:16 vertical
- Burned-in captions
- Export as MP4
- Versions for TikTok/Reels/Shorts if needed
- One revision round
- Delivery by Friday

The [hook](/glossary/video-hook-first-3-seconds) and [caption style](/glossary/burned-in-captions-meaning) usually deserve their own line, since they are where most revision requests come from.

If you need title suggestions, thumbnail frames, caption files, or upload copy, say so. Do not assume they are included.

## Feedback rules

Feedback can make or break the relationship. Give specific notes tied to timecodes.

Weak feedback:

> Make it pop more.

Useful feedback:

> At 0:03, cut the pause before "the real problem." At 0:12, the caption covers the product. At 0:22, remove the zoom because it makes the serious point feel too casual.

Agree on revision limits (and the [turnaround time](/glossary/video-editing-turnaround-time)) before work starts. One or two rounds are normal. Unlimited revisions create frustration for both sides.

## Create a reusable style guide

Once you have worked with a clipper for a few batches, turn repeated feedback into a short style guide. This saves time when you rehire, add a second editor, or scale production.

Your style guide can include:

- Caption font, size, and colour
- Safe zones for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts
- Intro hook rules
- How aggressive cuts should feel
- Music volume preferences
- Words or phrases to avoid
- How to handle brand names
- Examples of approved clips
- Examples of rejected clips

Keep it practical. A five-page brand manifesto is less useful than three approved clips with notes.

## Briefing for different content types

Adjust the brief by source:

| Source | Extra context to include |
| --- | --- |
| Podcast | Speaker names, timestamps, episode theme |
| Livestream | Sections to avoid, chat context, best moments |
| Gaming | Game, UI safe zones, meme style, audio tracks |
| Faceless video | Script, voiceover, stock rules, visual references |
| Product content | Claims, forbidden wording, brand assets |

The same editor may handle multiple formats, but they should not receive the same brief for every format.

## What to send after approval

Once you choose a clipper, send one clean package instead of scattered messages:

- Final source links
- The agreed brief
- Brand assets
- Examples
- Deadline
- Agreed pay and payment method (see [how much to pay clippers](/guides/how-much-to-pay-clippers) if you are still setting a rate)
- Feedback location

This makes the project feel professional from the first day. It also gives you one place to check if something goes wrong. Most revision problems start because instructions were split across DMs, comments, and old files.

## Free clipping brief template (copy and paste)

Copy this template into a doc, Notion page, or your first message to a new clipper. Fill in every bracket. If a line does not apply, write "n/a" so the clipper knows you did not forget it. You can also [download the clipping brief template](/templates/clipping-brief-template.txt) as a text file.

```text
CLIPPING BRIEF

Creator / channel: [name + links to your channels]
Batch name: [e.g. Episode 42 / Stream 2026-10-18]

1. GOAL
What these clips should achieve: [grow Shorts reach / drive full-episode views / test hooks]
Primary platform: [TikTok / Reels / YouTube Shorts / all]

2. AUDIENCE
Who watches: [age range, interests, what they already know about you]

3. SOURCE
Footage link: [folder or VOD link]
Length: [e.g. 62 minutes]
Transcript: [link or "none"]
Timestamps to clip: [00:04:10-00:05:20, 00:18:45-00:19:30, ... or "clipper picks"]
Sections to avoid: [sponsor reads, private comments, off-topic tangents]
Names and spellings: [speakers, brands, products]

4. DELIVERABLES
Number of clips: [e.g. 8]
Length per clip: [e.g. 30-60 seconds]
Aspect ratio: [9:16 vertical]
Export: [MP4, 1080x1920, 30fps]
Platform versions: [one master / separate TikTok, Reels, Shorts versions]
Extras: [title ideas / cover frames / caption text / none]

5. STYLE
Pacing reference: [link + what you like about it]
Caption reference: [link + font, size, color, highlight style]
What to avoid: [link + why]
Hook rule: [open on the strongest line, no intros or logos]
Music and SFX: [royalty-free only / my licensed library / none]
Brand assets: [logo, fonts, colors link]

6. RULES
Must include: [captions, CTA end card, watermark]
Never include: [words, topics, effects, copyrighted audio]

7. REVIEW
Deadline: [date and time zone]
Revision rounds: [1 or 2]
Feedback format: [timecoded notes in a shared doc / chat]
Approval: [send first 2 clips for style check before finishing the batch]
```

Save a filled-in copy after the first approved batch. Next time, you only need to update the source, timestamps, and deadline.

## FAQ

### Do I need a detailed brief for a trial batch?

Yes, but keep it short. A trial should test clipping skill, not the clipper's ability to guess your taste. Sections 1, 3, 4, and 5 of the template above are enough for a trial.

### What if I want the clipper to choose moments?

Say that explicitly and pay for source review. Give examples of what counts as a strong moment.

### Should the brief live in the job post?

Put the essentials in the job post, then share a fuller brief after you choose a clipper. The job post is for attracting the right applicants; the brief is for getting the work right.

### What is the next step?

Post a job with your scope and attach a simple brief. If you need help writing the job itself, use the [clipper job post template](/guides/how-to-write-a-clipper-job-post).
