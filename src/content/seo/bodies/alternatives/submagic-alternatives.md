# Submagic Alternatives: Other Caption Apps, AI Clippers or a Human Clipper

**The main Submagic alternatives are other AI caption apps such as CapCut, AI clipping apps such as OpusClip, or hiring a human clipper who picks the moments and edits each clip by hand.** Submagic is mainly a caption and quick-edit app for short videos. As of November 2026, paid plans start at $19 a month. People look elsewhere when they want cheaper captions, a different style, or someone to do the whole clipping job for them.

This page covers what Submagic does, what it costs, why people switch, and how each alternative compares.

## What Submagic does

Submagic is an AI editing app for short-form video. Its main job is captions. You upload a short video, and the app:

- **Adds animated captions** with emoji and keyword highlights
- **Cuts silences and bad takes** so the clip moves faster
- **Adds B-roll** (extra footage placed over the speaker) and sound effects
- **Writes a hook title** for the start of the clip

It also has a feature called Magic Clips that cuts a long video, such as a podcast, into several shorts. But most people use Submagic to finish a clip they already have. That is the key difference from apps like OpusClip, which are built to find clips inside long videos.

Captions added this way are part of the video image. See [burned-in captions](/glossary/burned-in-captions-meaning) for how they work and why short-form clips use them.

The video limit matters more than the price. If you post 3 clips a day, that is about 90 videos a month, which needs the Business plan.

## Why people look for Submagic alternatives

Submagic works well for many creators. These are the most common reasons people look elsewhere:

- **Price for captions only.** If you only need simple captions, a free editor can do it.
- **Video and length limits.** Starter covers 15 videos of up to 2 minutes. Longer videos need a higher plan.
- **Caption styles look alike.** Popular templates show up on many channels, so clips can blend in.
- **It doesn't pick your moments.** For most users, someone still has to find the best parts of a long video first.
- **Review time.** Auto captions get names, slang and game terms wrong. Someone has to check every clip.

## The alternatives

### Other caption and editing apps

These apps also add captions and quick edits. Switching can save money or give you a different style, but the work of picking moments stays with you.

- **CapCut:** a free editor with auto captions and templates. You cut the clip yourself, so it takes more time but gives you full control.
- **Your main editor:** Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve Studio can make captions from speech. They take longer to style but don't limit how many videos you caption.

### AI clipping apps

If you want the app to find clips inside long videos, look at AI clippers. They pick moments, reframe to 9:16 and add captions in one pass.

- **OpusClip:** the best-known long-video clipper. See [Opus Clip alternatives](/alternatives/opus-clip-alternatives) for its plans.
- **Vizard and Klap:** turn long videos into draft clips, similar to OpusClip.

We don't rank these apps or list their prices, because both change often. Check each app's own pricing page.

### Hire a human clipper

A clipper watches your content, picks the moments, and edits each clip by hand. That covers the hook, captions in your style, pacing, B-roll and [safe zones](/glossary/vertical-video-9-16-aspect-ratio). You get finished clips to post on your own channels.

Freelance clips typically cost **$15 to $35** each for simple talking-head or podcast edits, **$30 to $50** for mid-level work, and **$50 to $75 or more** for complex gaming or motion work. Monthly retainers run about $800 to $3,500 or more. See [clipper pay rates](/guides/clipper-pay-rates) for the full breakdown and [how much to pay clippers](/guides/how-much-to-pay-clippers) for budgets.

On Kreatli Marketplace, you post a job with the clips you need, and clippers apply. You agree pay and terms directly with the clipper. Kreatli doesn't process payments.

### Use both

Many clippers already use caption apps to speed up their work. You can hire a clipper and let them choose their own tools, or keep Submagic for quick everyday clips and hire a clipper for the ones that matter most.

**Worked example:** a podcast posts 10 clips a week, or about 40 a month. Submagic Pro covers 40 videos for $39 a month, but someone still has to find the moments, cut each clip and fix the captions. A clipper at $25 per simple clip costs about $1,000 a month for the same 40 clips, with no editing on your side. A split could look like this: 30 clips a month through Submagic, and 10 clips from a clipper for about $250.

## When AI is enough, and when to hire

**An AI app is often enough when:**

- You already cut your own clips and just need captions fast
- You post a few clips a week and have time to proofread them
- You're testing short-form and don't want to spend much yet

**Hire a clipper when:**

- You have long videos and no time to find the best moments
- Clips need judgement, such as gaming moments, humor or stories that need context
- You want a style that doesn't look like a popular template

## FAQ

### How much does Submagic cost?

As of November 5, 2026, Submagic's pricing page lists Starter at $19 per month, Pro at $39 per month and Business at $69 per month, per team member. Yearly billing lowers these to $12, $23 and $41 a month. A Custom plan is priced by sales. Check submagic.co/pricing for current prices.

### Is Submagic free?

Submagic's pricing page doesn't list a free plan. The site offers a free try with no card needed, but regular use needs a paid plan. If you need free captions, CapCut is the most common free editor with auto captions.

### Is Submagic worth it?

Submagic is worth it if you already have clips and want animated captions and quick edits fast. It's less useful if your real problem is finding the best moments in long videos or keeping a unique style, because you'll still do that work yourself. Try it on a few real clips before you pay.

### What is the best free Submagic alternative?

CapCut is the most common free option. It has auto captions and templates, but you cut the clip and style the captions yourself. Most other caption and AI clipping apps have a free tier with limits, so check each app's current plan.

### Is Submagic better than hiring a clipper?

They do different jobs. Submagic adds captions and quick edits to clips, for a monthly fee. A clipper picks the moments and edits the whole clip, for about $15 to $75 or more per clip. If you have time to find and cut clips yourself, Submagic may be enough. If you don't, a clipper saves that time.
