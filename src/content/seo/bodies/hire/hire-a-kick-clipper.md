## What does a Kick clipper do?

A Kick clipper reviews your stream recordings, finds moments that work for someone who was not watching live, and edits them into short vertical videos for other platforms. On a busy stream, that means sorting through hours of footage, chat reactions, and dead time to find the clips that actually carry your personality.

Common deliverables include:

- 15-60 second vertical clips for TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels
- Longer YouTube highlight videos or "best of the week" compilations
- Captioned reaction and IRL moments
- Clips cut from viewer-made Kick clips, cleaned up and reframed
- Timestamp logs so you can find moments again later

The editing itself is only part of the job. The rest is judgment: knowing which moment will land with a cold viewer and which one only made sense to chat at the time.

## Why Kick clipping needs its own approach

Kick has a strong clip culture. Viewers clip moments as they happen, and many streamers grow by having those moments spread across short-form platforms. That makes speed and volume important, but it also creates a few problems a good clipper knows how to handle.

### Posting rules differ by platform

Kick content does not always fit the rules of the platforms where clips get posted. Gambling segments, some IRL situations, and borrowed music can get clips limited or removed on TikTok, YouTube, or Instagram. A Kick clipper should know which segments to skip and flag anything risky before it goes on your channel.

### IRL and Just Chatting dominate

A lot of Kick content is IRL streaming and Just Chatting, where the payoff depends on setup, timing, and who else is on screen. Clippers need to keep enough context for the clip to make sense, cut everything else, and frame faces clearly in a 9:16 vertical crop.

### Speed matters

Stream moments go stale fast. Many streamers want clips within a day or two of going live, which means the clipper needs a reliable workflow for pulling VODs, logging moments, and exporting quickly.

## What to include in your Kick clipping job post

The clearer your brief, the more accurate the quotes you get. Use the [clipping job post template](/guides/how-to-write-a-clipper-job-post) if you want a structure to copy.

Include:

- Your Kick channel and main categories (IRL, Just Chatting, gaming, slots, mixed)
- How often you stream and typical stream length
- Number of clips or highlight videos you need per week
- Whether you or your mods will provide timestamps or community clip links
- Platforms you post to and any account handles
- Two or three past clips you liked
- Topics, people, or segments to avoid
- Caption style and how much meme editing you want

If your community already clips moments on Kick, say so. A clipper working from those clip links needs less review time than one starting from a full VOD.

## Pricing and pay models

Kick clipping is usually priced by clip or by batch, because review time is the biggest variable. A stream with timestamps costs less to clip than an eight-hour IRL stream the editor has to watch from scratch.

| Scope | Typical pricing logic |
| --- | --- |
| Clips from timestamps or community clips | Lower per-clip rate |
| Full VOD review | Higher batch rate |
| YouTube highlight video | Priced by finished length and complexity |
| Weekly or monthly stream package | Retainer billed per week or month |

Simple talking-head or reaction clips typically run around **$15-$35 per clip**. Gaming edits with sound design, memes, or motion graphics often land at **$50-$75+ per clip**. For benchmarks, see [how much to pay clippers](/guides/how-much-to-pay-clippers).

## How to evaluate Kick clipper samples

Ask for samples that match your content. An editor who is strong at fast gaming montages may not be the right fit for slow-building IRL moments.

Look for:

- A hook in the first two seconds
- Enough setup that a cold viewer understands the payoff
- Clean audio on loud reactions and crowded IRL scenes
- Captions that do not cover faces, chat, or key on-screen information
- Good moment selection, not just heavy effects
- Clean exports for Shorts, TikTok, and Reels with no watermarks

A small paid trial is the fastest way to compare clippers. Give two candidates the same segment from a recent stream and see who picks the better moments.

## Build a repeatable stream-to-clips workflow

Once you find a clipper you trust, make each handoff the same. A short checklist after every stream saves time on both sides:

- Stream date and VOD link
- Community clip links or mod timestamps
- Must-use and must-avoid moments
- Sponsor or brand notes
- Number of vertical clips and highlight videos
- Priority platforms and deadline

Ask your clipper to return a timestamp log with each batch. Over time it becomes a library of recurring bits, strong reactions, and segments that could become longer videos.

## Kick clipper vs Twitch clipper

The core skills are the same. The difference is context. A Kick clipper should understand Kick categories, IRL streaming, and which Kick segments are risky to post elsewhere. If you stream on both platforms, one editor can often handle both. See [hire a Twitch clipper](/hire/hire-a-twitch-clipper) for the Twitch version of this workflow.

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to hire a Kick clipper?

Simple vertical clips usually cost around $15-$35 each, and complex gaming or meme-heavy edits around $50-$75+. Full VOD review and YouTube highlight videos cost more. Many streamers switch to a weekly or monthly rate once the workflow is steady.

### Can a Kick clipper post clips for me?

On Kreatli, clippers deliver finished files and you post them on your own channels. Some streamers give a trusted editor scheduling access later, but that is a separate arrangement you agree on directly.

### Do I need to give my clipper timestamps?

No, but timestamps or community clip links lower the cost and speed up delivery. If you cannot provide them, ask the clipper to include full VOD review in the quote.

### Can my clipper handle gambling or slots segments?

A good clipper should flag them. Gambling content can be restricted on TikTok, YouTube, and Instagram, so agree up front on what can be posted and where.

### How fast can I get clips from a stream?

It depends on stream length and batch size. Many streamers aim for clips within 24 to 72 hours of going live. Faster turnaround is possible but usually costs more.
