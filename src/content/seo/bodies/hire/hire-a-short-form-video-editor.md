# Hire a Short-Form Video Editor

To **hire a short-form video editor**, post a job on **Kreatli Marketplace** describing your content, how many clips you need each week and your caption style. Short-form editors who specialize in TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels apply with samples, and you agree the pay upfront. Simple clips typically cost **$15 to $35 each**, and complex edits **$50 to $75 or more**.

On Kreatli, a short-form video editor is the same as a **clipper**: someone who turns your long videos, streams and podcasts into vertical clips that you post on your own channels. You pay for the editing, not for views. If you're weighing this against a full-time hire, see [freelance clipper vs. in-house editor](/compare/freelance-clipper-vs-in-house-editor).

## What does a short-form video editor do?

A short-form video editor makes vertical videos under about 90 seconds that hold attention on a phone. A good one handles:

- **Finding the moment:** picking the part of a long video that works on its own
- **The hook:** opening on the strongest line or visual, in the first 1 to 3 seconds ([more on hooks](/glossary/video-hook-first-3-seconds))
- **Pacing:** tight cuts with no dead air, using jump cuts, zooms and B-roll
- **Captions:** burned-in, easy to read with the sound off, in your brand style ([burned-in captions](/glossary/burned-in-captions-meaning))
- **Framing:** 9:16 vertical, with faces and text kept out of each app's [safe zones](/glossary/short-form-video-safe-zones)
- **Consistency:** the same look across every clip, week after week

### Short-form editor vs. long-form editor

| | Short-form video editor | Long-form video editor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | Vertical clips, usually 15 to 90 seconds | Full YouTube videos, ads, courses |
| Main skill | Hooks, pacing and captions for the scroll | Story structure, color, sound mix |
| Typical volume | 10 to 40 clips a week | 1 to 4 videos a week |
| How it's priced | Per clip, per batch or monthly | Per video or per hour |

Many editors do both, but hire for the work you need most. A great long-form editor isn't automatically good at hooks for TikTok.

## What to put in your job post

A clear brief gets you better applicants and accurate quotes. Include:

- **Your content:** podcast, streams, YouTube videos, courses, interviews or something else
- **Source length and schedule:** for example, "two 60-minute videos a week"
- **How many clips:** a firm number, or a range with a minimum
- **Clip length and platforms:** TikTok, Reels, Shorts, LinkedIn or all of them
- **Style:** caption font and colors, use of B-roll, zooms, music and sound effects
- **Examples:** two or three clips from other creators that you like
- **Timestamps:** whether you'll mark the moments, or the editor should find them
- **Turnaround and pay type:** per clip, per batch or monthly

The [clipper job post template](/guides/how-to-write-a-clipper-job-post) gives you a ready-made structure.

## How much does a short-form video editor cost?

| Scope | Typical price |
| --- | --- |
| Simple clip: talking head, podcast, captions | $15 to $35 per clip |
| Mid-level clip: B-roll, zooms, branded captions | $30 to $50 per clip |
| Complex clip: gaming sound effects, memes, motion graphics | $50 to $75+ per clip |
| Ongoing weekly volume | Monthly retainer, about $800 to $3,500+ |

You'll pay less when your source is predictable and you send timestamps. You'll pay more for rush delivery, heavy motion graphics, or long streams the editor has to watch in full. See [how much to pay clippers](/guides/how-much-to-pay-clippers) for budgets and examples.

## How to hire a short-form video editor

1. **Post your job** on Kreatli Marketplace with the brief above. Posting is free.
2. **Review samples:** look for vertical clips in your niche, not general showreels.
3. **Run a paid trial:** send one video and ask for 3 to 5 clips.
4. **Agree the scope and pay:** set a per-batch or monthly rate for ongoing work.
5. **Get finished clips:** review them, ask for changes and post them on your channels.

## How to vet a short-form video editor

When you review samples or a trial batch, check:

- **Does the clip make sense without context?** A new viewer should get the point in a few seconds.
- **Is the hook the strongest moment?** Clips that open with the setup lose viewers.
- **Are the captions accurate and readable?** Misspelled names are a red flag.
- **Is anything hidden behind the app's buttons?** Faces and text should stay clear of the edges.
- **Does it end cleanly?** A good clip ends on the payoff, not mid-sentence.
- **Did they follow your brief?** Length, style and platforms show how carefully they work.

For a full checklist, see [how to vet a clipper](/guides/how-to-vet-a-clipper).

## FAQ

### What is a short-form video editor?

A short-form video editor makes vertical videos under about 90 seconds for TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, usually by cutting clips from longer videos. They focus on the hook, fast pacing, captions and vertical framing.

### How much does it cost to hire a short-form video editor?

Simple clips typically cost $15 to $35 each, mid-level clips $30 to $50, and complex edits with motion graphics or gaming effects $50 to $75 or more. Creators who need clips every week usually move to a monthly retainer, about $800 to $3,500 or more.

### Where can I hire a short-form video editor?

You can post a job on Kreatli Marketplace, which is built for hiring clippers and short-form editors. General freelance sites like Upwork and Fiverr also have short-form editors, but you'll sort through more general video editors there.

### Is a short-form video editor the same as a clipper?

Mostly, yes. "Clipper" usually means someone who cuts short clips from longer content, which is the main job of a short-form editor. Some short-form editors also create original short videos from raw footage. See [what is a clipper](/glossary/what-is-a-clipper).

### Should I hire a freelancer or an in-house short-form editor?

Freelancers are easier to start with and you only pay for the clips you need. An in-house editor makes sense once you need clips every day and want someone fully inside your team. Compare both in [freelance clipper vs. in-house editor](/compare/freelance-clipper-vs-in-house-editor).
