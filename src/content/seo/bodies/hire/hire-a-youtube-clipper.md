YouTube Shorts rewards volume and retention, but cutting 15–30 vertical clips per week from long-form uploads is a full-time job on its own. You pay **agreed project, hourly, or monthly rates** for finished clips delivered to **you** for your YouTube channel (and other platforms if you specify). This is clipper hire, not a per-view clipping campaign.

## What does a YouTube Shorts clipper do?

A YouTube clipper repurposes your long-form content (tutorials, vlogs, video podcasts, livestream VODs) into vertical clips built for the Shorts feed. They focus on:

- **Retention structure**: pattern interrupts, text hooks, and cut density in the first few seconds ([retention rate explained](/glossary/video-retention-rate-meaning))
- **9:16 reframing**: centering subjects, keeping safe zones clear of UI overlays, and never cropping critical text ([vertical video specs](/glossary/vertical-video-9-16-aspect-ratio))
- **Chapter and timestamp literacy**: pulling segments that make sense without the full video
- **Shorts-native captions**: large, high-contrast burned-in text for viewers watching muted
- **Batch consistency**: the same caption style, end behavior, and brand colors across a week of uploads

Unlike a general YouTube editor who mostly works in 16:9 long-form, a Shorts clipper thinks in **daily or weekly clip quotas**.

## What to include in your YouTube clipping job post

A good job post saves you from sorting through dozens of irrelevant applications. Include:

- **Channel niche and link**: so clippers can see your voice and audience
- **Source type**: long-form uploads, streams, or raw footage, and the average length
- **Weekly volume**: target number of Shorts per week
- **Moment selection**: whether you will send timestamps or expect the clipper to find moments
- **Hook examples**: 2–3 Shorts (yours or others') that match the pacing you want
- **Caption and brand rules**: fonts, colors, and anything to avoid
- **Extras**: titles, descriptions, or cover frames if you want them
- **Pay type and availability**: per project, hourly, or monthly; project-based, part-time, or full-time

## How to hire a YouTube clipper

1. **Post your job brief**: share your niche, average long-form length, target Shorts per week, and examples of hooks you like.
2. **Review clipper profiles**: prioritize portfolios with Shorts or vertical samples, not only cinematic reels.
3. **Run a trial batch**: 5–10 clips from one recent video.
4. **Agree scope and price**: commit to a weekly pipeline once quality and turnaround are proven.
5. **Receive finished clips**: review, request revisions, and upload to YouTube on your schedule.

If you are new to hiring, the [step-by-step guide to hiring a clipper](/guides/how-to-hire-a-clipper) walks through each stage. Channels already producing at volume should read [how to scale YouTube Shorts production](/guides/scale-youtube-shorts-production).

## How to vet a Shorts clipper

When you review samples or a trial batch, look past effects and check the fundamentals:

- **Standalone context**: Shorts viewers often find clips away from your channel, so each clip needs to make sense on its own.
- **Hook strength**: the opening line or visual should give a reason to keep watching.
- **Caption accuracy and placement**: no typos, and nothing hidden behind the Shorts UI.
- **Pacing**: tight without cutting off natural speech or punchlines.
- **Moment selection**: did they pick the clips you would have picked, or find better ones?
- **Consistency**: do ten clips look like they came from the same channel?

A paid trial with the same source footage for two candidates makes the difference in judgment easy to see.

## FAQ

### Can a Shorts clipper also handle my long-form YouTube videos?

Some can, but clipping and long-form editing are different skills. For Shorts at scale, hire someone whose samples show **vertical retention editing**. If you need one person for everything, say so in your job post and expect a higher rate or longer turnaround.

### How do I brief a clipper on which moments to clip?

Send **timestamps or chapter markers** when you have them, plus 2–3 example Shorts that match your target pacing. Many creators share a spreadsheet with the video link, must-clip moments, and "do not use" sections such as sponsor reads.

### How much does it cost to hire clippers for YouTube Shorts?

Simple talking-head Shorts typically cost **$15–$35 per clip**, while gaming or motion-heavy Shorts run **$50–$75+**. High-volume channels often switch to a weekly or monthly rate.

### Should Shorts clippers post on my channel?

On Kreatli Marketplace, clippers **deliver files** and you keep publishing control. That keeps your channel login, analytics, and monetization under your account. If you want upload and scheduling help, mention it in the brief, since not every clipper offers it.

### How is this different from AI clipping tools?

AI tools generate fast drafts from transcripts. Human clippers curate **brand voice, joke timing, and visual polish** that automated drafts can miss.
