## What does a podcast clipper do?

A podcast clipper watches your full episodes (video, or audio with a static video feed), finds high-retention moments, and edits them into vertical shorts. Strong podcast clippers understand:

- **Hook-first pacing**: the best quote or tension beat lands in the first 2–3 seconds ([why the first 3 seconds matter](/glossary/video-hook-first-3-seconds))
- **Talking-head framing**: jump cuts, punch-in zooms, and caption timing that keep eyes on screen
- **Episode context**: which guest lines stand alone without the full conversation
- **Brand consistency**: your caption style, lower-thirds, and cover frames week over week

They are not general long-form editors. Their skill is repurposing audio-first content into short-form discovery. For a deeper look at the format, read [what podcast clipping means](/glossary/podcast-clipping-meaning).

## What to include in your podcast clipping job post

A clear job post gets you relevant applicants and accurate quotes. Before you post, write down:

- **Show format**: solo, two-host, guest interview, panel, or video-first podcast
- **Episode length and frequency**: for example, "one 75-minute episode every Tuesday"
- **Source files**: whether you can share separate camera angles and audio tracks, or only a single mixed file
- **Clips per episode**: a firm number, or a range with a minimum
- **Clip length**: most podcast clips sit between 30 and 90 seconds
- **Caption style**: font, color, word highlighting, emoji or none
- **Platforms**: TikTok, Reels, Shorts, LinkedIn, or all of them
- **Timestamps**: whether you will mark moments or expect the clipper to review the full episode
- **No-go zones**: sponsor reads, off-the-record segments, or guest topics you do not want clipped
- **Pay type**: per project, hourly, or monthly, plus your preferred payment method

## Podcast clipper pricing

Podcast clips are usually one of the more affordable clipping formats because the footage is predictable. Pricing still depends on review time and edit complexity.

| Scope | Typical price |
| --- | --- |
| Simple talking-head clip with captions | $15–$35 per clip |
| Multi-cam clip with B-roll or motion graphics | $50–$75+ per clip |
| Full episode review plus 8–15 clips | Quoted as a batch |
| Weekly pipeline for 2–4 episodes | Monthly retainer |

Timestamps you provide lower the cost, because the clipper spends less time hunting for moments. If your show runs weekly, a monthly rate is easier to manage than per-clip invoices. ## How to hire a podcast clipper

1. **Post your job brief**: describe your show format, ideal clip length, caption style, and how many clips per episode you want.
2. **Review clipper profiles**: look for podcast or interview clipping samples, not general wedding or travel reels.
3. **Run a paid trial**: send one episode and ask for 3–5 clips to check moment selection and caption style.
4. **Agree scope and price**: set a per-batch rate or a monthly rate for an ongoing pipeline.
5. **Receive finished clips**: download the vertical files, request revisions, and publish on your channels.

The [step-by-step guide to hiring a clipper](/guides/how-to-hire-a-clipper) covers each stage in more detail.

## How to vet a podcast clipper

Podcast clipping looks simple, which is why moment selection separates good clippers from average ones. When you review samples or a trial batch, check:

- **Does the clip make sense cold?** A viewer who has never heard your show should understand the point within a few seconds.
- **Is the hook the strongest line?** Clippers who open with the setup instead of the payoff lose viewers.
- **Are captions accurate?** Misspelled guest names and brand terms are a red flag.
- **Is the audio clean?** Levels should be even between hosts and guests, with no clipped peaks.
- **Does the ending land?** Good clips end on a payoff or a loop, not mid-sentence.
- **Did they follow the brief?** Caption style, length, and no-go zones show how carefully they work.

Ask how they would handle a quiet episode with fewer standout moments. A thoughtful answer tells you more than a flashy portfolio. For a full checklist, see [how to vet a freelance video editor](/guides/how-to-vet-a-clipper).

## FAQ

### How many clips should I get per podcast episode?

Most interview shows aim for **8–15 clips per hour-long episode**. Solo commentary podcasts might ship **5–10**. Start with a smaller trial batch of about 5 clips to align on hook style before scaling to a full episode or weekly pipeline.

### Do podcast clippers need video, or is audio enough?

Video is strongly preferred, because even a static wide shot with captions usually holds attention better than audio-only on TikTok and Reels. If you only have audio, say so in your job post. Some clippers add waveform visuals or stock B-roll, but that changes scope and price.

### How much does it cost to hire a podcast clipper?

Simple talking-head podcast clips typically cost **$15–$35 per clip**, while multi-cam clips with B-roll or motion graphics run **$50–$75+**. Weekly shows often move to a monthly rate once the workflow is stable.

### Should I hire one podcast clipper or rotate freelancers?

Creators scaling past one episode per week usually **stick with one clipper** who learns guest names, recurring bits, and your caption rules. Kreatli Marketplace supports ongoing relationships, so you can post a part-time or full-time job once you find the right fit.

### Is this the same as content-rewards clipping?

No. Content-rewards programs pay clippers based on views on **their** accounts. On Kreatli Marketplace, you hire a clipper for **agreed pay** and clips go to **your** channels. Same word "clipper," different economics. See [what is a clipper](/glossary/what-is-a-clipper) for the full breakdown.
