# Safe Zones for TikTok, Reels and Shorts Explained

A **safe zone** is the part of a vertical video frame that stays visible after the app adds its own buttons, captions, usernames, and progress bars on top. When editors talk about the **safe zone on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts**, they mean the central area where text, faces, and logos will not be covered. Anything placed outside it may be hidden or hard to read in the feed.

## Why it matters

Short-form apps play video full screen and then layer their interface over it. That interface is not part of your file, so you cannot see it in your editing software. A caption that looks perfect on your timeline can end up under the like button or behind the post description on a phone.

For **clippers**, safe zones decide whether captions, hooks, and on-screen text actually get read. For **creators**, they protect the parts of a clip that carry the message, such as a guest's face, a product, or a call to action.

Getting safe zones wrong usually means:

- Captions hidden behind the username and description
- Hook text covered by the right-side button column
- Faces cropped by the app's top bar or tabs
- Logos or watermarks lost at the edges

## Where the interface usually sits

Each app has its own layout, and layouts change, but the problem areas are similar across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts:

| Area | What usually covers it | What to keep out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bottom | Username, post caption, audio label, progress bar | Captions, key text, faces |
| Right side | Like, comment, share, and other action buttons | Text, small details |
| Top | Search, tabs, or status bar | Hook text, faces |
| Center | Mostly clear | Your main subject and captions |

The bottom area is often the largest no-go zone, because post descriptions can expand and push the overlay up. That is why many editors place **burned-in captions** around the middle of the frame rather than at the very bottom. See [burned-in captions](/glossary/burned-in-captions-meaning) for more on caption placement.

## What the platforms say

Exact safe zone sizes are not always published for regular organic posts, and third-party templates often disagree with each other. Some official guidance does exist on the advertising side.

As of 2026, TikTok's Ads Help Center publishes downloadable safe zone files for some ad formats. For its TopView placement, TikTok notes that the in-feed safe zone is more restrictive than the open-screen one, that the safe zone size depends on caption length and add-ons, and that it may not cover all devices. TikTok recommends previewing ads to check placement.

Those points carry over to organic clips as a rule of thumb: longer descriptions take more space, and devices differ. Treat any single pixel template as a starting point, not a guarantee.

## How editors work with safe zones

1. **Edit in 9:16.** Start with a correctly sized [vertical video](/glossary/vertical-video-9-16-aspect-ratio) frame, usually 1080×1920.
2. **Use an overlay.** Many editors place a transparent safe zone guide over the timeline while editing, then hide it before export.
3. **Keep captions central.** Put captions roughly in the middle of the frame, clear of the bottom and right edges.
4. **Frame faces high but not too high.** Leave room above the head for the top bar, and keep faces above the bottom overlay.
5. **Keep the hook readable.** Opening text should sit fully inside the safe area, since it carries the [first 3 seconds](/glossary/video-hook-first-3-seconds).
6. **Preview on a phone.** Upload as a draft or private post and check it in each app before publishing.

## One layout for three apps

## FAQ

### What is the safe zone on TikTok?

It is the central area of a 9:16 video that the app's buttons, username, and caption do not cover. Keep key text and faces away from the bottom and right edges, and preview in the app because the space taken by the caption changes with its length.

### Are safe zones the same on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts?

They are similar but not identical, and each app updates its layout over time. Designing for the area that is clear on all three is the simplest way to post one clip everywhere.

### Where should captions go in a vertical video?

Most editors place captions around the middle of the frame, above the bottom overlay and away from the right-side buttons. Test with a private post to confirm nothing is covered.
