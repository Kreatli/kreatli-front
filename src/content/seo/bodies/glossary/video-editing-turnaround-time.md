# What Is Turnaround Time in Video Editing?

**Turnaround time** is how long an editor takes to deliver finished work after receiving the footage and brief. In short-form clipping, video editing turnaround time is usually measured in hours or days, for example "clips delivered within 48 hours of each upload."

It's one of the most important terms to agree before work starts, because a great clip delivered after the moment has passed is worth a lot less.

## Why it matters for creators and clippers

Short-form content is time-sensitive. A stream highlight or podcast hot take often performs best while the topic is still current. Many creators aim to post clips within 24–72 hours of the original upload or stream.

- **For creators:** turnaround determines your posting schedule and how quickly long-form content gets repurposed
- **For clippers:** turnaround determines how many clients you can take on and what you can charge
- **For both:** a clear deadline prevents the most common freelance frustration, which is mismatched expectations about "soon"

## What turnaround time includes

Turnaround should be defined precisely. Agree on:

1. **Start point:** when the clock starts, such as when footage is uploaded, when the brief is confirmed, or when the stream ends
2. **End point:** first draft delivered, or final approved files
3. **Revision turnaround:** how fast edits come back after feedback, often shorter than the first delivery
4. **Working days:** whether weekends count
5. **Time zone:** especially when creator and clipper are in different regions

"48 hours" means different things if it counts from a Friday night upload and excludes weekends.

## Typical turnaround ranges

These are typical market expectations, not fixed rules. Actual timelines depend on the editor's workload and the complexity of the clip.

| Type of work | Typical turnaround |
| --- | --- |
| Simple talking-head clip with captions | Same day to 48 hours |
| Batch of podcast clips from one episode | 1–3 days |
| Stream highlights from a long VOD | 1–3 days, depending on VOD length |
| Clips with heavy [motion graphics](/glossary/motion-graphics-short-form-video) | 2–5 days |
| Rush or same-day delivery | Hours, usually at a higher rate |

Reviewing source footage takes time too. Finding the best moments in a six-hour stream is a real part of the job, which is why Twitch and Kick work often takes longer than clipping a 30-minute podcast. See [hire a Twitch VOD editor](/hire/hire-a-twitch-clipper) for how creators set these expectations.

## What affects turnaround time

- **Source length:** more footage means more review time
- **Clip count:** 3 clips versus 15 clips from the same source
- **Complexity:** captions only versus custom animation and sound design
- **Revisions:** unclear briefs lead to more rounds and longer total timelines
- **Editor capacity:** clippers working for several clients schedule around existing commitments
- **Feedback speed:** delays on the creator's side push back final delivery

A clear brief is the easiest way to shorten turnaround. Our guide on [how to brief a video editor](/guides/how-to-brief-a-clipper) covers what to send up front.

## How turnaround affects price

Faster delivery usually costs more. Rush work means the editor has to reshuffle other clients or work outside normal hours. Retainers often include a guaranteed turnaround, such as "within 24 hours of each episode," priced into the monthly fee. If you're setting rates, see [how to price video editing services](/guides/how-to-price-video-editing-services).

## Examples

**Podcast retainer:** The creator uploads each episode by Monday 10 a.m. The clipper delivers eight clips by Wednesday 10 a.m., with revisions returned within 24 hours.

**Streamer:** Highlights are due within 36 hours of the stream ending so the creator can post while chat is still talking about it.

**One-off project:** A creator orders 20 clips from an archive with a one-week deadline and no rush.

## FAQ

### What is a good turnaround time for short-form clips?

For simple clips, 24–48 hours from receiving footage is a common expectation. Larger batches or graphic-heavy clips typically take two to five days. The right turnaround is the one both sides agree on in writing and the editor can reliably hit.

### How do I write turnaround time in a job post?

Be specific about start point, end point, and working days, for example "8 clips delivered within 48 hours of each upload, weekdays only, one revision round within 24 hours." On Kreatli Marketplace, include it in your job description when you [post a clipping job](/jobs/create).

### Is rush delivery worth paying extra for?

It can be for time-sensitive content like reactions, news, or live event highlights. For evergreen content, standard turnaround is usually fine and cheaper. Agree the rush rate before you need it.

### What should a clipper do if they can't meet a deadline?

Tell the creator as early as possible, explain why, and propose a new delivery time or partial delivery. Consistently hitting turnaround is one of the main things creators mention in reviews, so it directly affects future work.
