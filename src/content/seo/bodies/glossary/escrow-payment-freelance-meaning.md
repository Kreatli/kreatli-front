# What Is Escrow Payment (Freelance Marketplace Term)?

An **escrow payment** is money a client deposits with a neutral third party, which holds it until the agreed work is delivered and approved. In freelance marketplaces, the escrow payment freelance meaning is simple: the client funds the job up front, the platform or escrow service holds the funds, and the freelancer is paid when the milestone or project is accepted.

Escrow aims to protect both sides. The freelancer knows the money exists, and the client knows it won't be released until the work arrives.

**Important:** Kreatli Marketplace does **not** offer escrow and does not process payments. Creators and clippers agree payment terms directly with each other. The rest of this page explains how escrow works in general and how to protect yourself without it.

## Why it matters for creators and clippers

Payment risk runs in both directions in freelance video work:

- **Clippers** worry about delivering clips and never getting paid
- **Creators** worry about paying up front and getting late, poor, or no work

Escrow is one answer to that trust gap. It is not the only one, and many freelance relationships run on clear agreements, staged payments, and reputation instead.

## How escrow works on platforms that offer it

1. **Agreement:** client and freelancer agree on scope, price, and milestones.
2. **Funding:** the client deposits the amount for the first milestone or the full project.
3. **Delivery:** the freelancer completes and submits the work.
4. **Approval:** the client approves, or requests revisions within the agreed scope.
5. **Release:** the held funds are paid to the freelancer, usually minus platform fees.
6. **Disputes:** if the two sides disagree, the platform's dispute process decides what happens to the funds.

Some large general freelance marketplaces build escrow into fixed-price contracts. Their fees, release timing, and dispute rules differ, so check their current terms. ## Practical protections without escrow

### Agree a written scope

Put the details in the chat before work starts: number of clips, length, style, caption requirements, revision rounds, deadline, rate, and payment method. A written record prevents most disputes. Our guide on [how to brief a video editor](/guides/how-to-brief-a-clipper) covers what to include.

### Use milestones

Split larger jobs into stages and pay per stage, for example per batch of five clips. A [milestone payment](/glossary/milestone-payment-freelance) limits how much either side has at risk at any moment.

### Ask for or offer a deposit

For new relationships, a partial upfront deposit (often a portion of the first batch) shows commitment from the creator and reduces risk for the clipper.

### Start with a paid test

A single paid test clip lets both sides check quality, communication, and payment reliability before committing to volume.

### Use watermarked previews

Clippers can share low-resolution or watermarked previews for approval, then send final files once payment for that stage clears.

### Check reviews and profiles

Kreatli lets creators leave reviews when a job completes, and clipper profiles are reviewed by the Kreatli team. Read past reviews and portfolios, and see [how to vet a freelance video editor](/guides/how-to-vet-a-clipper) for more checks.

### Choose payment methods with care

Some payment methods offer buyer or seller protections and some don't. Understand the terms of whichever method you agree on before sending or accepting money.

## Example

A creator hires a clipper for 20 clips a month at a per-project rate. They agree in chat to four weekly batches of five clips, paid by bank transfer within two days of each batch's approval, with a paid test clip first. Neither side ever has more than one week's work at risk.

## FAQ

### Does Kreatli Marketplace use escrow?

No. Kreatli Marketplace does not offer escrow or process payments. Creators state their pay type and payment preferences in the job post, and both sides agree terms and pay directly. Use written scope, milestones, deposits, and reviews to protect yourself.

### Is escrow safe for freelancers?

Escrow on an established platform generally reduces non-payment risk because funds are committed before work starts. Disputes are still decided by the platform's rules, and fees apply. Read the dispute policy before relying on it.

### How do I avoid not getting paid as a freelance clipper?

Agree scope and payment in writing, start with a paid test, ask for a deposit on new relationships, and deliver in batches with payment per batch. Watermarked previews help too. If you're new, [how to become a clipper](/guides/how-to-become-a-clipper) covers setting terms from the start.

### What happens to escrow money in a dispute?

On platforms that offer escrow, the funds usually stay held while the platform reviews evidence from both sides, then are released, refunded, or split according to its rules. The process and timelines vary by platform, so check their current terms.
