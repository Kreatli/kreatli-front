# What Are Burned-In Captions?

**Burned-in captions** are subtitles that are permanently rendered into the video image itself, so they appear on every platform and cannot be turned off. The burned in captions meaning is the opposite of closed captions, which live in a separate file (like an SRT) that viewers can switch on or off. Burned-in captions are also called **open captions** or **hardcoded subtitles**, and they are standard on TikTok, Shorts, and Reels clips.

Once exported, the text is part of the pixels, just like the speaker's face.

## Why it matters

A large share of short-form viewing happens with sound off or low, in public places, or while scrolling quickly. Captions let viewers follow the clip without audio and help them catch the hook immediately.

Burned-in captions also give editors control. You choose the font, size, color, position, and timing, which means captions can become part of a channel's visual identity rather than a generic overlay.

For **creators**, captions are one of the simplest ways to improve accessibility and retention. For **clippers**, caption style is often one of the first things clients judge in a portfolio.

## Burned-in captions vs. closed captions

| Factor | Burned-in (open) captions | Closed captions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the text lives | Inside the video image | Separate file or platform track |
| Can viewers turn them off? | No | Yes |
| Styling control | Full control by the editor | Limited, set by the player |
| Works on every platform | Yes, travels with the file | Depends on platform support |
| Editable after export | No, requires a re-export | Yes, edit the file |
| Search and indexing | Text is not machine-readable as captions | Platforms can read the text |

Many creators use both: burned-in captions for style and muted viewing, and an uploaded caption file or platform auto-captions where supported for accessibility and search. Check each platform's current caption features.

## How burned-in captions are made

1. **Transcribe the audio.** Most editing apps can auto-transcribe, but the text needs a human check for names, slang, and errors.
2. **Split into short lines.** Short-form captions usually show a few words at a time, sometimes one word, so they are easy to read on a phone.
3. **Style them.** Pick a bold, legible font, add outline or shadow for contrast, and highlight key words in color.
4. **Position inside the safe zone.** Keep captions away from the bottom and right edges where app buttons appear. See [vertical video and 9:16 safe zones](/glossary/vertical-video-9-16-aspect-ratio).
5. **Time to speech.** Word-by-word or phrase timing that matches the speaker's rhythm.
6. **Export.** Rendering the video burns the captions into the frames.

### Common caption styles

- **Word-by-word pop:** Each word appears as it is spoken, popular in podcast and commentary clips
- **Phrase blocks:** Two to five words at a time, easier to read for slower speech
- **Keyword highlight:** Important words in a contrasting color or larger size
- **Minimal:** Clean white text with a subtle shadow, often used for brand or educational content

## Examples

**Podcast clip:** Bold white word-by-word captions with yellow highlights on key terms, centered between two stacked speakers in a [talking-head edit](/glossary/talking-head-edit).

**Gaming clip:** Short phrase captions above the gameplay so they do not cover the action.

**Brand clip:** Clean captions in the brand's font and colors, consistent across every post.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between burned-in captions and subtitles?

Subtitles is a general term for on-screen text of speech. Burned-in captions are subtitles that are permanently part of the video image. Subtitles can also be delivered as a separate file that viewers toggle on or off.

### Are burned-in captions good for accessibility?

They help viewers who are deaf, hard of hearing, or watching muted, since the text is always visible. However, viewers cannot resize or restyle them, and the text is not machine-readable. Using clear, high-contrast styling and also providing platform captions where possible is a stronger approach.

### Do burned-in captions help short-form videos perform better?

They make clips easier to follow without sound and can help viewers catch the hook immediately, which supports retention. Results vary by audience and content, so compare clips with and without captions on your own channel.

### Can you remove burned-in captions?

Not easily. Because the text is part of the image, removing it usually means re-exporting from the original project without captions. Keep your caption-free master file if you might need a clean version later.
