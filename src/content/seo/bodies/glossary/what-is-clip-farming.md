# What Is Clip Farming?

**Clip farming** means producing and posting large numbers of short clips with the main goal of collecting views, followers, or view-based payouts. If you are wondering "what is clip farming," the term has two common meanings. In streaming culture, it describes streamers or channels who stage moments, or repost other people's highlights, to farm views. In the clipping economy, it describes clippers who mass-produce clips for paid campaigns that pay per 1,000 views.

Both meanings share one idea: volume first, with the clip treated as a unit of reach.

## Why it matters

The phrase gets used as both a strategy and an insult, so context matters.

- **For streamers and creators**, "clip farming" can be a criticism: acting out for the camera to manufacture viral moments, or a channel reposting your highlights without adding anything.
- **For clippers**, it often just describes the job: cutting many clips quickly to earn from [clipping campaigns](/glossary/what-is-a-clipping-campaign).
- **For brands**, clip farming is a distribution tactic. Many accounts posting the same campaign footage can reach far more people than one channel.

Understanding which sense someone means helps you decide whether to embrace it, avoid it, or hire for something different.

## Meaning 1: Farming clips on streams and channels

In the streaming world, clip farming usually refers to one of these:

- **Staged moments.** A streamer exaggerates reactions, picks fights, or does stunts mainly so viewers will clip them.
- **Highlight reposting.** A channel downloads popular moments from other creators and reposts them with little editing, hoping to catch algorithm traffic.
- **Compilation channels.** Pages that stitch together many short moments from one or more creators, often across gaming and IRL streaming.

Some of this is harmless and even welcomed. Many streamers want fans to clip them because it spreads their content. The problems start when reposting crosses into using someone else's footage without permission or in ways a platform treats as unoriginal. Whether you need permission depends on the creator and platform rules. Our guide on [whether you need permission to clip streams](/guides/do-you-need-permission-to-clip-streams) covers this as general information, not legal advice.

## Meaning 2: Clip farming in clipping campaigns

In the clipping economy, clip farming describes clippers working campaigns at volume:

1. Join a campaign that pays per 1,000 views (see [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping))
2. Cut many clips from the sponsor's footage
3. Post across several accounts and platforms
4. Collect payouts on clips that clear the minimum view threshold

This is a legitimate model with real upside. It also carries volatility. Most clips earn little, a few break out, and per-clip caps limit how much any single clip can pay. Campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**, with caps and thresholds that vary by sponsor.

## Clip farming vs. freelance clip editing

| Factor | Clip farming (campaigns) | Freelance clip editing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Maximum views per hour | Quality clips for one creator |
| Who posts | The clipper | The creator |
| Pay | Per verified view | Fixed per clip or period |
| Editing depth | Often fast and template-driven | Tailored to the creator's style |
| Income | Variable | Predictable once agreed |

On **Kreatli Marketplace**, clippers are hired to deliver finished clips to a creator, who posts them on their own channels at agreed pay. Freelance clips typically run **$15 to $35** for simple talking-head edits and **$50 to $75+** for complex gaming or motion work. Many clippers farm campaigns and take freelance jobs at the same time.

## Examples

**Streaming sense:** A channel posts 20 unedited clips a day from a popular streamer's VODs with no captions or context. Viewers and the streamer call it clip farming.

**Campaign sense:** A clipper spends a weekend cutting 30 clips from a podcast campaign, posts them on three TikTok accounts, and earns on the five that clear the view threshold.

**Freelance alternative:** The same clipper takes a part-time job editing 10 Shorts a week for a streamer at $40 per clip, delivered as files the streamer posts.

## FAQ

### Is clip farming bad?

Not inherently. Fans clipping a streamer and campaign clippers posting sponsor footage are both normal parts of the creator economy. It gets criticized when moments are staged purely for clips or when someone reposts another creator's content without permission or added value.

### Can you make money clip farming?

Yes, through paid clipping campaigns and, in some cases, platform monetization on your own accounts. Earnings are variable because pay depends on views, minimum thresholds, and caps. Many clippers pair it with freelance work for steadier income.

### What is a clip farmer?

A clip farmer is someone who produces clips at high volume mainly to collect views or view-based pay. The term can describe a campaign clipper, a compilation channel, or a streamer who stages moments to get clipped.

### Is clip farming allowed on TikTok and YouTube?

Posting clips is allowed, but platforms have rules on reused or unoriginal content, and monetization programs may exclude it. Rules change, so check each platform's current policies. Adding real editing, captions, and context generally helps.
