# What Are Verified Views in Clipping?

**Verified views** are the views a clipping campaign actually counts toward your payout after the platform or sponsor filters out views it considers invalid. In verified views clipping, the number on your TikTok or Shorts dashboard is not always the number you get paid on. Campaigns may exclude bot traffic, paid or incentivized views, views from non-eligible regions, or views on clips that broke the campaign rules.

If a campaign pays per 1,000 views, verified views are the "views" in that formula.

## Why it matters

For **clippers**, verified views decide your real income. A clip showing 80,000 views might pay on far fewer if part of that traffic does not qualify, or if the clip was rejected for missing a required tag.

For **sponsors**, verification protects the budget. Without it, a campaign could pay out for fake engagement that never reached a real audience.

Understanding verification helps you:

- Estimate earnings more realistically before you start editing
- Avoid tactics that get clips disqualified
- Compare campaigns on effective pay, not headline rates

## How view verification works

Each platform uses its own method, and most do not publish every detail. The general pattern looks like this:

1. **Account connection.** Clippers link their social accounts so the platform can read view counts, usually through official platform data or tracking tools.
2. **Submission and approval.** The sponsor or platform reviews each clip against campaign rules. Unapproved clips earn nothing.
3. **Filtering.** Views flagged as bots, purchased, or otherwise suspicious are excluded. As of 2026, Whop states that its Content Rewards counts only legitimate views and excludes bot or paid-inducement traffic.
4. **Counting window.** Some campaigns only count views within a set period after posting or approval.
5. **Payout.** Verified views are multiplied by the rate, then limited by any [payout cap](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning) or remaining budget.

### What commonly gets excluded

| Excluded view type | Why it is filtered |
| --- | --- |
| Bot or automated traffic | Not a real audience |
| Purchased views or engagement | Violates campaign and platform rules |
| Views from rule-breaking clips | Missing tags, links, or required mentions |
| Views outside the counting window | Campaign period ended |
| Views from non-eligible regions or platforms | Sponsor targets specific audiences |

Exact rules vary by sponsor, so read each campaign's terms before posting. Our guide on [how to verify clip views](/guides/how-to-verify-clip-views) explains what sponsors check and how to document your numbers.

## Verified views in practice

**Example 1:** A clip shows 50,000 views. The campaign verifies 42,000 after excluding non-eligible traffic. At $1.50 per 1,000 views, the payout is $63, not $75.

**Example 2:** A clipper forgets the required on-screen mention. The clip is rejected, so none of its views count, no matter how well it performed.

**Example 3:** A clip goes viral after the campaign's counting window closes. Views after that date do not add to the payout.

Campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**, but effective pay depends on how many views survive verification. See [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping) for the full calculation.

## Verified views vs. fixed-pay clipping

Verification only matters when pay is tied to views. On **Kreatli Marketplace**, clippers are hired by creators at agreed per-project or per-period pay and deliver finished files to the creator, who posts them. Kreatli does not track views, and the clipper's pay does not depend on view counts. Creators may still watch retention and views to judge which edits work, but that is performance feedback, not payroll.

Many clippers run view-based campaigns and take fixed-pay jobs at the same time. If you want steadier work, browse [clipping jobs](/clipping-jobs) alongside campaigns.

## FAQ

### Why are my verified views lower than my actual views?

Campaigns usually exclude views they consider invalid, such as bot traffic, views from non-eligible regions, or views outside the counting window. Clips that miss a required rule may be rejected entirely. Check the campaign's terms to see what it counts.

### How do clipping platforms verify views?

Most connect to your social accounts to read view data, then review clips and filter suspicious traffic. Methods differ by platform and are not always fully published. If a campaign's verification rules are unclear, ask before you invest time in it.

### Can buying views help in clipping campaigns?

No. Purchased or incentivized views are typically excluded and can get your clips rejected or your account removed from campaigns. They can also break the social platform's own rules.

### Do freelance clippers get paid on verified views?

Not usually. Freelance clippers hired on Kreatli Marketplace are paid a fixed amount per clip, project, or period, regardless of how the clip performs after the creator posts it.
