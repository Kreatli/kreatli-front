# Vertical Video / 9:16 Aspect Ratio Explained

**Vertical video** is video shot or edited to be taller than it is wide, and the standard shape is a **9:16 aspect ratio video**: 9 units wide for every 16 units tall. The most common resolution is **1080×1920 pixels**, which fills a phone screen held upright. TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels are all built around 9:16, so it is the default format for short-form clips.

It is simply 16:9 widescreen turned on its side.

## Why it matters

Most long-form content, including YouTube videos, streams, and podcasts, is recorded in 16:9. Turning that into short-form means converting it to 9:16 without losing the subject, the action, or readability. That conversion is a large part of a clipper's job.

Getting the format right affects:

- **Full-screen display.** Video that is not 9:16 may show black bars or get cropped by the app.
- **Readability.** Captions and on-screen text need to sit where app buttons will not cover them.
- **Perceived quality.** Low-resolution or poorly reframed clips look out of place in the feed.

## Common resolutions

| Resolution | Aspect ratio | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1080×1920 | 9:16 | Standard for TikTok, Shorts, Reels |
| 720×1280 | 9:16 | Lower-resolution exports, faster uploads |
| 2160×3840 | 9:16 | 4K vertical, larger files |
| 1080×1350 | 4:5 | Instagram feed posts |
| 1080×1080 | 1:1 | Square posts |
| 1920×1080 | 16:9 | Standard YouTube, streams, podcasts |

For most clips, **1080×1920 at 30 or 60 fps** in MP4 (H.264 video, AAC audio) is a safe export setting.

## Platform specs (as of 2026)

Platform limits change often, so treat these as a starting point and check each platform's current help pages before a big upload.

- **YouTube Shorts:** Videos that are square or vertical can be Shorts. YouTube extended the maximum length from 60 seconds to **3 minutes** starting October 15, 2024.
- **Instagram Reels:** Instagram raised the Reels upload limit to **3 minutes** in January 2025, up from 90 seconds.
- **TikTok:** Supports 9:16 at 1080×1920 and allows videos well beyond short-form length. Upload and in-app recording limits differ, so check current limits in the app.

## Safe zones

Each app overlays buttons, captions, usernames, and progress bars on top of your video. The **safe zone** is the central area where text and faces will not be covered.

General guidelines used by many editors:

- Keep key text and faces away from the **bottom area**, where usernames, captions, and music labels appear
- Leave room on the **right side** for the like, comment, and share column
- Avoid placing captions in the **top strip**, where some apps show search or tabs

Platforms do not all publish exact pixel values for safe zones, and third-party templates differ. A practical approach is to keep captions roughly in the middle third of the frame and test with each app's preview before posting.

## How clippers convert 16:9 to 9:16

- **Center crop with tracking.** Cropping the frame and moving it to follow the speaker or action.
- **Split screen.** Stacking two speakers or a facecam above gameplay.
- **Blurred or branded background.** Placing the 16:9 frame in the middle with a fill above and below. Quick, but uses less of the screen.
- **Reframe per shot.** Re-cropping each cut for the best composition. Slower, better results.

These choices are central to [talking-head edits](/glossary/talking-head-edit) and gaming highlights alike. Combined with [burned-in captions](/glossary/burned-in-captions-meaning) placed inside the safe zone, they make a clip feel native to the feed.

## Examples

**Podcast:** A 16:9 two-camera recording becomes a stacked split screen with captions between the speakers.

**Gaming stream:** The facecam goes in the top third, cropped gameplay fills the bottom two-thirds.

**Solo creator:** A centered crop follows the speaker, with occasional zooms.

## FAQ

### What is the best resolution for 9:16 video?

1080×1920 is the standard for TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels. It balances quality and file size. 4K vertical (2160×3840) works but rarely adds visible benefit on a phone feed.

### Is 9:16 the same as 1080×1920?

1080×1920 is one resolution that uses the 9:16 ratio. 720×1280 and 2160×3840 are also 9:16. The ratio describes shape, the resolution describes how many pixels fill it.

### Can I upload 16:9 video to TikTok or Shorts?

You can, but it will usually show with bars or appear small in a vertical feed. On YouTube, landscape uploads are not treated as Shorts. Converting to 9:16 generally gives a better viewing experience.

### What is the safe zone for vertical video?

It is the central area of the frame not covered by app buttons, usernames, and captions. Keep key text and faces away from the bottom and right edges, and preview on each platform since layouts differ and change over time.
