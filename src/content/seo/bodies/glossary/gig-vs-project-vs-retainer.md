# Gig vs. Project vs. Retainer: What's the Difference?

**Gig, project, and retainer** are three common ways to structure freelance work. A **gig** is a small, one-off task with a fixed scope. A **project** is a larger piece of work with defined deliverables and an end date. A **retainer** is an ongoing arrangement where the client pays a recurring fee for a set amount of work or availability each period.

Understanding gig vs project vs retainer helps creators choose the right way to hire a clipper, and helps clippers price and plan their workload.

## Why it matters for creators and clippers

The structure you pick shapes almost everything else:

- **Pricing:** per task, per project, or a recurring monthly amount
- **Commitment:** a single trial versus months of work together
- **Workflow:** ad hoc requests versus a predictable weekly pipeline
- **Income stability:** clippers with retainers can plan; clippers living on gigs often can't

Most creator-clipper relationships move through all three: a small gig to test fit, a project to prove the workflow, then a retainer once both sides are happy.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Gig | Project | Retainer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | One small task | Defined set of deliverables | Ongoing work each period |
| Duration | Hours to days | Days to weeks | Monthly, open-ended |
| Pricing | Fixed per task | Fixed per project or per milestone | Recurring fee (per month or year) |
| Commitment | Low | Medium | High |
| Example | Edit one test clip | 20 clips from a podcast season | 25 clips per week, every week |
| Best for | Trials, quick fixes | Launches, backlogs | Regular uploads and streams |

## Gigs

A gig is the smallest unit of freelance work. The term is common on marketplaces built around packaged services, where a freelancer offers something like "one 60-second clip with captions" at a set price. Compare how this works on [Kreatli vs. Fiverr](/compare/kreatli-vs-fiverr).

Gigs are good for testing an editor's style or handling a single urgent clip. They're less efficient for regular work because every order restarts the briefing process.

## Projects

A project has a defined outcome: a batch of clips, a highlights package from one event, or repurposing a back catalog. Pricing is usually per project or per clip, often split into [milestone payments](/glossary/milestone-payment-freelance).

Projects suit creators with a specific goal or a backlog to clear. For clippers, they're bigger than gigs but still end, so pipeline planning matters.

## Retainers

A retainer is a recurring agreement, such as a set number of clips every week for a monthly fee. Retainers typically cost less per clip than one-off work because the editor gets steady volume and already knows the style.

For context, freelance clip retainers typically range from about **$800 to $3,500+ per month** depending on volume, niche, and complexity. Our guide on [how much to pay clippers](/guides/how-much-to-pay-clippers) breaks this down further.

Retainers suit podcasters, streamers, and channels with regular uploads. Agree the monthly volume, turnaround, revision rounds, and what happens to unused capacity.

## Examples

**Gig:** A YouTuber pays a clipper for one test Short from a recent upload to check caption style.

**Project:** A podcaster hires the same clipper to cut 30 clips from a finished 10-episode season, paid in three milestones.

**Retainer:** After the project, the podcaster moves to a monthly retainer for eight clips per weekly episode.

## FAQ

### Is a retainer better than per-project pay?

Not always. Retainers give clippers stable income and creators lower per-clip costs and reliable capacity, but they need consistent content to work. If you upload irregularly, per-project pay is usually simpler. Many creators start per project and switch to a retainer once volume is steady.

### What is a retainer in freelance video editing?

It's a recurring arrangement where a creator pays a set fee, usually monthly, for an agreed amount of editing, such as 20 clips a week. The editor reserves capacity for that client and typically works from an established style and brief.

### What's the difference between a gig and a project?

Size and scope. A gig is a single small task, like one clip. A project is a larger body of work with several deliverables and milestones, like a full batch of clips from a podcast season.
