# What Is a VOD? (Video on Demand for Streamers and Clippers)

A **VOD**, short for *video on demand*, is a recorded video that people can watch whenever they choose instead of at a scheduled time. If you are asking **what is a VOD** in streaming, it usually means the saved recording of a past livestream, which Twitch calls a **past broadcast**. For clippers, the VOD is the raw footage that clips, highlights, and Shorts are cut from.

## Why VODs matter

For **streamers**, a VOD lets viewers who missed the live show catch up, and it keeps a record of what happened on stream. It is also the starting point for almost every kind of repurposing, from YouTube uploads to vertical clips.

For **clippers**, the VOD is where the work begins. Watching a stream live and clipping in real time is possible, but most editing happens afterward, from the recording. A good VOD workflow decides how fast clips go out and how many moments get caught.

VODs also expire on some platforms. If no one saves or cuts the footage in time, those moments are gone for good.

## How VODs work on major platforms

Platform rules change, so check current help pages before you rely on a storage window.

### Twitch

As of 2026, Twitch's help center says past broadcasts are only saved when the streamer has **Store past broadcasts** turned on. Storage time depends on the account:

- **7 days** for most broadcasters
- **14 days** for Affiliates
- **60 days** for most Partners, and for Prime and Turbo users

After that, past broadcasts are deleted automatically. Streamers can turn parts of a VOD into **Highlights**, which stay up longer. Since 2025, though, Twitch limits Highlights and uploads to 100 hours of storage per channel.

### YouTube

YouTube archives live streams on the channel automatically after they end, as long as the stream is under 12 hours. Longer streams may not be fully captured, and YouTube recommends keeping a local recording as a backup.

## VOD vs. clip vs. highlight

| Term | What it is | Typical length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VOD / past broadcast | Full recording of a livestream | Hours |
| Highlight | Edited selection of moments from a VOD | Minutes |
| Clip | One short moment, often for social posting | Seconds to a minute or two |
| Upload | A video added to the channel separately from a stream | Any length |

See [what a highlight reel is](/glossary/highlight-reel-meaning) for how highlights are put together.

## How clippers work from a VOD

A typical VOD-to-clips workflow looks like this:

1. **Get the footage early.** The streamer shares the file, or the clipper saves the VOD with permission before it expires.
2. **Find the moments.** Streamer timestamps, chat spikes, and community-made clips all point to the best parts.
3. **Cut and reframe.** Each moment is trimmed and converted from 16:9 to [9:16 vertical video](/glossary/vertical-video-9-16-aspect-ratio).
4. **Add captions and a hook.** [Burned-in captions](/glossary/burned-in-captions-meaning) help viewers who watch muted.
5. **Deliver or post.** A hired clipper sends finished files to the creator. A campaign clipper posts on their own account.

Using someone else's VOD without permission is risky. Our guide on [whether you need permission to clip streams](/guides/do-you-need-permission-to-clip-streams) covers the basics.

## FAQ

### What does VOD stand for?

VOD stands for video on demand. On streaming platforms it usually means the saved recording of a livestream that viewers can watch after it ends. Twitch calls these past broadcasts.

### How long do Twitch VODs last?

As of 2026, Twitch keeps past broadcasts for 7 days for most broadcasters, 14 days for Affiliates, and 60 days for most Partners and Prime or Turbo users. They are only saved if the streamer has the storage setting turned on, so check the channel's settings and Twitch's help center for current rules.

### Can I clip someone else's VOD?

Only with permission, unless you are clipping your own streams. A VOD is the streamer's content, and it may include music or game footage owned by others. Work for the streamer, join an official campaign, or get written permission first.
