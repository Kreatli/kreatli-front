# What Is a Clipping Campaign?

A **clipping campaign** is a paid promotion where a creator, brand, or app funds a budget and invites many clippers to cut short clips from its content, post them on their own TikTok, Shorts, Reels, or X accounts, and get paid based on the views those clips earn. If you are asking "what is a clipping campaign," the short answer is: distributed, view-based promotion. Rewards are usually quoted as a rate per 1,000 views (CPM), with caps and minimum view thresholds attached.

It is a different arrangement from hiring a freelance clip editor, where you pay a fixed amount for finished clips that go on your own channel.

## Why it matters

The word "clipping" now covers two jobs, and campaigns are the reason. For **creators and brands**, a campaign buys reach across dozens or hundreds of accounts you do not own. For **clippers**, it is a way to earn from editing without a client, but income depends on how the algorithm treats each post.

Knowing how campaigns work helps you:

- Judge whether a campaign budget will actually buy the reach you want
- Read campaign rules before spending hours editing
- Decide when a fixed-pay editor is the better fit for your goals

## How a clipping campaign works

Most campaigns follow the same basic flow, whether they run on a content-rewards platform or a Discord server:

1. **The sponsor sets terms.** This includes the total budget, the rate per 1,000 views, accepted platforms, content rules, and often a [payout cap](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning) and minimum view threshold.
2. **Clippers pull source material.** They cut moments from the sponsor's streams, podcasts, or videos, or use provided assets.
3. **Clippers post on their own accounts.** The clip lives on the clipper's profile, not the sponsor's.
4. **Submissions get reviewed.** The sponsor or platform approves or rejects each clip against the rules.
5. **Views are counted and paid.** Only views that meet the platform's rules count as [verified views](/glossary/verified-views-meaning). Payout is calculated until the clip hits its cap or the budget runs out.

As of 2026, Whop's Content Rewards documentation describes this structure directly: sponsors set a reward rate per 1,000 views, a total budget, a minimum payout before a clip reaches review, and an optional maximum payout per video. Other platforms use similar levers. Check each platform's current terms, since rules change often.

### Common campaign settings

| Setting | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| Rate per 1,000 views | How much each verified view is worth |
| Total budget | When the campaign stops paying |
| Minimum view threshold | Whether a clip earns anything at all |
| Per-clip cap | The most one clip can earn |
| Content rules | Required tags, links, mentions, length, or style |
| Accepted platforms | Where clips must be posted to count |

Campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**, though rates vary widely by niche and sponsor. See [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping) for how the math works.

## Clipping campaign vs. hiring a clipper

| Factor | Clipping campaign | Hiring a freelance clipper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who posts | Clippers, on their own accounts | You, on your own channels |
| How pay works | Per verified view, with caps | Fixed per clip, project, or month |
| What you get | Distributed reach | Finished files and a consistent style |
| Predictability | Variable for both sides | Agreed upfront |
| Best for | Launches, awareness, wide reach | Building your own Shorts, TikTok, Reels presence |

On **Kreatli Marketplace**, creators post a job, hire a clipper, and receive finished clips to publish themselves. Pay is per project or period, not per view. Many clippers run campaigns for upside and take fixed-pay jobs for stable income, so the two models often sit side by side.

## Examples

**Podcast launch campaign:** A podcast funds a $2,000 budget at $1.50 per 1,000 views with a per-clip cap. Forty clippers post. A few clips break through, most earn little, and the budget runs out in three weeks.

**Owned-channel pipeline:** The same podcast instead hires one clipper at $25 per clip for 15 clips a week. Every clip goes on the show's own TikTok and Shorts, building one audience in one place.

## FAQ

### How do clipping campaigns pay clippers?

Most pay per 1,000 verified views at a rate the sponsor sets, after the clip is approved. Many add a minimum view threshold before a clip earns anything and a cap on how much one clip can earn. Payment timing and platform fees vary, so read the campaign terms first.

### Are clipping campaigns legit?

Many are legitimate, and established platforms run them with clear rules. Risks include vague terms, slow or rejected payouts, and campaigns that run out of budget early. Our guide on [whether clipping is legit](/guides/is-clipping-legit) covers red flags to check.

### How much does a clipping campaign cost to run?

You set the total budget, so the cost is whatever you fund plus any platform fees. What matters more is how many verified views that budget buys at your chosen rate and caps. Estimate it before launch rather than raising the rate mid-campaign.

### Is a clipping campaign better than hiring an editor?

They solve different problems. A campaign spreads your content across other people's accounts for reach, while a hired clipper builds your own channels with consistent output. If you want the second, you can [post a clipping job](/jobs/create) on Kreatli Marketplace.
