# RPM vs. CPM: What's the Difference for Creators and Clippers?

**RPM vs. CPM** comes down to who the number describes. On YouTube, **CPM** is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube takes its share, while **RPM** (revenue per mille) is what the creator actually earns per 1,000 views after that share. In clipping campaigns, CPM means something else again: the rate a sponsor pays a clipper per 1,000 views.

## Why it matters

The two numbers are easy to confuse, and mixing them up leads to bad estimates.

For **creators**, CPM tells you how much advertisers value your audience. RPM tells you what you take home. Planning income from CPM will overstate your earnings.

For **clippers**, the word "CPM" in a campaign brief is not the same as YouTube's CPM. A campaign CPM is your pay rate, not an advertiser metric, and it is not affected by YouTube's revenue share at all.

## YouTube RPM and CPM, defined

As of 2026, YouTube's help center defines the terms like this:

- **CPM** is the cost per 1,000 ad impressions, measured before YouTube's revenue share. YouTube also reports **playback-based CPM**, which is what advertisers pay per 1,000 playbacks where an ad was shown.
- **RPM** is how much a creator has earned per 1,000 video views. It includes revenue from several sources, including ads, channel memberships, YouTube Premium revenue, Super Chat, and Super Stickers.

YouTube gives two reasons RPM is usually lower than CPM:

1. RPM is calculated **after** YouTube's revenue share.
2. RPM counts **all views**, including views where no ad was shown.

For Shorts, YouTube calculates RPM using **engaged views**. For long-form videos, it uses all views.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | YouTube CPM | YouTube RPM | Clipping campaign CPM |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who it describes | Advertisers | Creator earnings | Clipper pay rate |
| Per 1,000 of what | Ad impressions | Views (engaged views for Shorts) | Views that the campaign counts |
| Before or after platform share | Before | After | Set by the sponsor |
| Includes non-ad revenue | No | Yes | No |
| Who sees it | Creator in YouTube Analytics | Creator in YouTube Analytics | Clipper in campaign terms |

## How clipping CPM differs

In a [clipping campaign](/glossary/what-is-a-clipping-campaign), a sponsor sets a rate per 1,000 views and pays clippers directly, often through a content rewards platform. That rate is usually called CPM, but it works more like a piece rate than an ad metric.

Clipping CPM has its own limits:

- Only [verified views](/glossary/verified-views-meaning) usually count
- A clip may need to clear a [minimum view threshold](/glossary/minimum-view-threshold-meaning) before it earns
- A [payout cap](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning) can stop earnings on a viral clip
- The campaign budget can run out

See [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping) for the full formula and examples.

## Example

A podcast channel sees a $10 CPM on a long-form video in YouTube Analytics. Its RPM for the same video might be much lower, because YouTube's share comes out and some views had no ads.

Separately, the podcast funds a clipping campaign at $2 CPM. A clipper whose clip earns 50,000 counted views is paid $100, regardless of the podcast's YouTube RPM. The two numbers have no direct connection.

## Where fixed-pay clipping fits

The creator sets the pay when posting the job, per project, hourly, monthly, or yearly, and the clipper delivers files for the creator's own channels. Any RPM those clips generate belongs to the creator. Kreatli does not run CPM campaigns.

## FAQ

### Why is my YouTube RPM lower than my CPM?

YouTube says RPM is calculated after its revenue share and includes all views, even ones that did not show an ad. CPM only measures what advertisers paid per 1,000 ad impressions before that share.

### Which matters more for creators, RPM or CPM?

RPM, for income planning, because it reflects what you actually earned per 1,000 views. CPM is useful for understanding how advertisers value your audience and how it changes by topic or season.

### Is clipping CPM the same as YouTube CPM?

No. Clipping CPM is the rate a sponsor pays a clipper per 1,000 counted views. YouTube CPM is what advertisers pay YouTube per 1,000 ad impressions. They share a name but measure different things.
