# Royalty-Free Music Licensing for Clips, Explained

**Royalty-free music** is music you license once (or through a subscription) and can then use in your videos without paying a royalty each time the video is played. For **clips** on TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts, royalty-free music for clips usually comes from a stock music subscription, a free library with clear terms, or the audio library built into each platform.

"Royalty-free" does not mean "free" or "no rules." Every track still comes with a license, and that license decides where and how you can use it. This page is general information, not legal advice. Check the actual license for any track you use.

## Why it matters for creators and clippers

Music is a common reason clips get muted, blocked, or claimed. Getting it right protects:

- **The creator's channel:** repeated claims or strikes can limit reach or monetization
- **Brand deals:** sponsored content often needs commercially cleared audio
- **The clipper's reputation:** delivering clips that get muted on upload means rework and unhappy clients

Because a clipper on Kreatli Marketplace delivers finished files to the creator, who then posts them, both sides need to agree on **who chooses the music and whose license covers it**. Put that in the job brief.

## Key licensing terms to know

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Royalty-free | Pay once or subscribe; no per-play royalties. Still licensed, with terms. |
| Copyrighted / commercial track | Popular songs owned by labels and publishers. Using them outside platform tools usually requires permission. |
| Public domain | No copyright applies. Note that a specific recording of an old song may still be protected. |
| Creative Commons | Free use under conditions, such as giving credit or no commercial use. Read the exact license type. |
| Sync license | Permission to pair music with video. Royalty-free subscriptions typically include this for covered uses. |
| Content ID / claims | Automated systems that match audio and can mute, block, or redirect revenue. |

## Where clip music typically comes from

### Platform audio libraries

Each major platform offers music you can add inside the app or studio, for example YouTube's Audio Library in YouTube Studio, TikTok's Commercial Music Library for business use, and Meta's Sound Collection for Facebook and Instagram. These are often the safest choice for content posted on that same platform, but the terms usually apply only there. A track cleared inside one app is not automatically cleared for another platform or an ad. Check each library's current terms as of 2026.

A practical point: many popular trending sounds are available to personal accounts but may be restricted for business accounts or branded content.

### Stock music subscriptions

Paid libraries license tracks across platforms, usually tied to the subscriber's channels. If a clipper uses **their own** subscription for a client's clips, confirm the license allows client work. Some plans cover only the subscriber's channels.

### Free libraries and original music

Free sites often require attribution or ban commercial use, so read the terms before using them on a monetized or sponsored channel. Original or commissioned music removes most licensing questions at a higher upfront cost.

## How to handle music in a clipping workflow

1. **Decide who supplies music.** Options: creator provides tracks, clipper sources from the creator's subscription, or clipper uses their own license that permits client work.
2. **Consider delivering without music.** Many creators add trending sounds in-app at upload, so the clipper delivers clean files with voice and sound effects only.
3. **Keep a license record.** Note track name, source, and license for each clip.
4. **Watch the source footage.** Background music in a stream or podcast recording can trigger claims even if you add nothing. Streamers playing copyrighted music is a common issue when you [clip Twitch streams](/guides/get-paid-to-clip-twitch-streams).
5. **Respect permission for the footage itself.** Music is only one layer. See [do you need permission to clip streams](/guides/do-you-need-permission-to-clip-streams) for the footage side.

Include your music rules when you [write a video editing job post](/guides/how-to-write-a-clipper-job-post) so applicants can confirm they can work within them.

## Examples

**Podcast channel:** The creator holds a stock music subscription and shares an approved playlist. The clipper uses only those tracks at low volume under speech and logs each track in the delivery notes.

**Brand account:** The brand posts to TikTok as a business account, so the clipper delivers without music and the social team adds audio from the Commercial Music Library at upload.

## FAQ

### Is royalty-free music free to use?

Not necessarily. Royalty-free means you don't pay per play, but you may pay a one-time fee or subscription, and the license still sets conditions. Some royalty-free tracks are free with credit; others are paid. Always read the specific license.

### Can I use trending TikTok sounds in my clips?

Usually you can add sounds inside TikTok's app for that post, subject to TikTok's terms and your account type. Business and branded accounts often have a narrower selection. Exporting a trending song into a clip file and uploading it elsewhere is a different situation and often not covered.

### Who is responsible if a clip gets a copyright claim?

It depends on your agreement and the platform's rules. The channel posting the clip is the one that receives the claim, so creators should set music rules in the brief and clippers should follow them and record sources. This is general information, not legal advice.
