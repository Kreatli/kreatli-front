# CPM Meaning in Clipping (and on Whop): Pay per 1,000 Views

**CPM in clipping means how much you earn for every 1,000 views your clip gets.** A $1 CPM pays $1 per 1,000 views, so a clip with 50,000 views earns $50. CPM stands for *cost per mille*, Latin for "per thousand." Clipping campaigns on Whop and similar platforms commonly advertise about **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**.

CPM pay is tied to views, not to the editing work. That is the opposite of hiring a freelance clipper on **Kreatli Marketplace**, where the pay is agreed before the work starts and doesn't change with views.

## How CPM pay works in clipping

A typical CPM (or "content rewards") campaign works like this:

1. A brand or creator funds a **budget** and sets a **rate per 1,000 views**.
2. Clippers edit the campaign footage and **post it on their own accounts**.
3. The platform counts the views on each clip and filters out bots and paid views.
4. Each approved clip earns **(views ÷ 1,000) × rate**, until the budget runs out.

**Worked example:** a clip gets 80,000 views on a $1.50 CPM campaign.
80,000 ÷ 1,000 = 80, and 80 × $1.50 = **$120**. That's only if the clip passes the campaign's minimum, is approved, and stays under its payout cap.

## CPM on Whop clipping campaigns

Whop Content Rewards is a widely used CPM clipping platform, which is why many people search "CPM meaning in Whop." As of 2026, Whop's documentation describes these settings for each campaign:

- **Reward rate:** the CPM, set by the business. Whop's terms use "$1 per 1000 views" as an example.
- **Minimum payout:** a clip must earn a minimum amount before it is sent for review. Whop's example: at $3 per 1,000 views with a $6 minimum, a clip needs about 2,000 views. See [minimum view threshold](/glossary/minimum-view-threshold-meaning).
- **Maximum payout:** a cap on what one video can earn. At $2 per 1,000 views with a $500 cap, a clip stops earning at 250,000 views. See [payout caps](/glossary/payout-cap-meaning).
- **Approval:** the business approves or rejects each clip against its rules, and only legitimate views count. See [verified views](/glossary/verified-views-meaning).
- **Budget:** approved clips are paid until the campaign's budget runs out or it ends.

Whop's terms also set a 10% Content Reward Fee, charged to the business that runs the campaign. Rates vary by campaign, so always read a campaign's own rules before you post. For how Whop compares with hiring a clipper, see [Whop clipping vs Kreatli](/compare/kreatli-vs-whop-clips) and [what content rewards are](/glossary/content-rewards-meaning).

## Why CPM pay is unpredictable

The rate is only part of the story. What you take home depends on:

- **Views:** most clips get few views, and a few clips get most of them
- **Minimums:** clips below the minimum earn nothing
- **Caps:** a viral clip can stop earning at the cap
- **Budget:** a campaign can run out of money before your clip is paid
- **Approval:** a clip that breaks the rules can be rejected

**Example:** you post 10 clips at $1 per 1,000 views with a 2,000-view minimum. Six get under 2,000 views and earn $0. Three get 8,000 views each and earn $24 together. One gets 120,000 views and earns $120. That's **$144 for 10 clips**, or $14.40 each.

## CPM vs. fixed pay per clip

| | CPM / content rewards | Fixed pay (Kreatli Marketplace) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you're paid for | Views on your posts | The finished clips |
| Who posts the clip | You, on your own account | The creator, on their channel |
| How predictable | Low | High: the price is agreed upfront |
| Typical rate | About $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views | $15 to $35 per simple clip, $50 to $75+ for complex work |
| Best for | Clippers who are good at getting views | Clippers who want steady income |

A freelance clipper charging $25 per podcast clip knows the job pays $25 once it's delivered. A CPM clipper has no such guarantee. See [clipper pay rates](/guides/clipper-pay-rates) for both models in more detail.

## FAQ

### What does CPM mean in clipping?

CPM in clipping is the amount you earn per 1,000 views on a clip you post. At a $1 CPM, a clip with 10,000 views earns $10. Most campaigns also have a minimum view count, a payout cap and a budget limit.

### What does CPM mean on Whop?

On Whop Content Rewards, CPM is the reward rate a business pays per 1,000 views on approved clips. As of 2026, Whop's settings also include a minimum payout before a clip is reviewed and a maximum payout per video, so the rate alone doesn't tell you what a clip will earn.

### Is a $1 CPM good for clipping?

A $1 CPM is about average. Campaigns commonly advertise $0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views. At $1, you need 100,000 views to earn $100, so what matters most is whether your clips can get views, plus the campaign's minimum and cap.

### How do you calculate CPM earnings?

Divide the views by 1,000 and multiply by the rate. For example, 250,000 views at $2 CPM is 250 × $2 = $500. Then check the campaign's payout cap, because earnings stop there.

### Do creators pay CPM when hiring a clipper?

Not on Kreatli Marketplace. When you hire a clipper there, you agree a fixed price for the clips, and the clipper doesn't earn per view on your channel. CPM only applies to content-rewards campaigns, where clippers post on their own accounts.

### Can clippers do both CPM campaigns and freelance work?

Yes. Many clippers take fixed-pay jobs for steady income and join a few CPM campaigns for upside. Treat CPM as a bonus and fixed pay as your base.
