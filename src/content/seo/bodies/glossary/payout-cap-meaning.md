# What Is a Payout Cap in Clipping Campaigns?

A **clipping payout cap** is the maximum amount a campaign will pay, either for a single clip, a single clipper, or the campaign as a whole. Once a cap is reached, extra views stop earning money even if the clip keeps going viral. Most view-based campaigns pair caps with a **minimum view threshold**, the floor a clip must clear before it earns anything.

Caps and thresholds together define the real earning range of a campaign, often more than the headline rate per 1,000 views does.

## Why it matters

For **clippers**, a cap limits upside. A clip that hits 5 million views might pay the same as one that hits 1 million if the cap kicks in early. Thresholds mean many low-performing clips earn nothing.

For **sponsors**, caps protect the budget. Without them, one viral clip could drain an entire campaign and leave nothing for the other clippers.

Before you join or fund a campaign, check:

- What the per-clip cap is, if any
- Whether there is a limit per clipper or per account
- How large the total budget is and how fast it might run out
- What minimum views a clip needs to qualify

## Types of payout caps

### Per-clip cap

The most common type. It limits what one clip can earn. As of 2026, Whop's Content Rewards documentation describes a "maximum payout" setting that caps how much a creator can earn from one video. Example: at $2 per 1,000 views with a $500 cap, a clip stops earning at 250,000 verified views.

### Per-clipper cap

Some campaigns limit total earnings per clipper or per account, or limit how many submissions each person can make. This spreads the budget across more people and discourages one clipper from flooding the campaign.

### Campaign budget cap

Every campaign has a total budget. When it is spent, payouts stop for everyone, including clips still gaining views. A well-performing campaign can exhaust its budget faster than clippers expect.

### Minimum view threshold

Not a cap, but the other half of the equation. Many campaigns require a minimum number of views, or a minimum payout amount, before a clip is reviewed or paid. Whop's documentation, for example, describes a minimum payout that a video must reach before it enters the sponsor's review queue.

| Rule | What it limits | Who it protects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per-clip cap | Earnings from one clip | Sponsor budget |
| Per-clipper cap | Earnings or submissions per person | Fair budget spread |
| Campaign budget | Total spend | Sponsor |
| Minimum view threshold | Which clips earn at all | Sponsor review time |

## How caps change your earnings

Campaigns commonly advertise roughly **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**. Combined with caps and thresholds, that creates a band where clips actually pay.

**Example:** A campaign pays $1.50 per 1,000 views, requires 10,000 views minimum, and caps clips at $300.

- Clip A: 6,000 views earns $0, below the threshold
- Clip B: 80,000 views earns $120
- Clip C: 3 million views earns $300, capped at 200,000 views

Use the [clipping earnings calculator](/tools/clipping-earnings-calculator) to model scenarios, and read [CPM meaning in clipping](/glossary/cpm-meaning-clipping) for the base math. Payouts are also calculated on [verified views](/glossary/verified-views-meaning), which may be lower than your dashboard shows.

## Caps vs. fixed-pay clipping

Fixed-pay clipping has no view caps because pay is not tied to views. On **Kreatli Marketplace**, creators hire clippers at agreed per-project, hourly, or monthly pay, and the clipper delivers finished files for the creator to post. Freelance clips typically run **$15 to $35** for simple talking-head edits and **$50 to $75+** for complex gaming or motion work. The trade-off is no viral upside, but no zero-pay clips either.

## FAQ

### What happens when a clip hits its payout cap?

The clip stops earning for that campaign, even if views keep growing. The views still help your account, but they no longer add to the payout. Check whether a campaign has a cap before you choose which clips to push.

### Why do clipping campaigns have payout caps?

Caps stop one viral clip from consuming the whole budget. That keeps money available for more clippers and gives the sponsor wider distribution for the same spend.

### What is a minimum view threshold?

It is the number of views, or minimum earnings, a clip needs before it qualifies for review or payment. Clips below the threshold usually earn nothing. Thresholds vary by campaign, so read the terms first.

### How should sponsors set a payout cap?

Start from your total budget and how many clippers you want earning from it. A cap low enough to spread the budget, but high enough that strong clips still feel rewarded, tends to keep clippers engaged. Model a few scenarios before launch.
