# What Is a Clipper? The Job, the Pay, and How to Start

A **clipper** is a person who turns long videos, such as streams, podcasts and YouTube uploads, into short vertical clips for TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. Clippers find the best moments, cut them down to under a minute, and add captions and a strong hook. They get paid in one of two ways: a **fixed fee per clip** from the creator who hires them, or **pay per 1,000 views** when they post clips on their own accounts in a CPM campaign.

## What does a clipper do?

A clipper's job is to take one long video and get many short ones out of it. A typical day looks like this:

- **Watch or scan the source video.** A three-hour stream or a one-hour podcast might hold five to fifteen moments worth posting.
- **Pick the moments.** Good clips have a clear start, a payoff, and a reason to keep watching. A funny reaction, a hot take or a clutch play all work.
- **Cut and reframe.** The clipper trims the moment and turns the wide video into a tall 9:16 frame so it fills a phone screen. See [vertical video and the 9:16 aspect ratio](/glossary/vertical-video-9-16-aspect-ratio).
- **Add captions and a hook.** Most short-form viewers watch with the sound off, so captions matter. The first few seconds have to stop the scroll. See [the video hook and the first 3 seconds](/glossary/video-hook-first-3-seconds).
- **Deliver or post.** A freelance clipper sends the finished files to the creator. A campaign clipper posts the clips on their own accounts.

Most clippers use simple tools. CapCut is the most common starting point, and Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve are popular once the work gets more complex. Our guide to [the best video editing software for clippers](/guides/best-video-editing-software-for-clippers) compares them.

## What is a clipper in video editing vs. on social media?

You will see "clipper" used in two places, and they mean slightly different things.

**In video editing**, a clipper is an editor who specializes in short-form repurposing. They are not making long YouTube videos or ads. They make the short clips that come out of long content, and they are judged on hooks, pacing and captions.

**On social media**, "clipper" usually means someone in the clipping economy. These are people who post clips of streamers, podcasters or brands on TikTok and Shorts, often as part of a paid campaign. When people say "clippers grew this streamer's audience," this is the kind they mean.

The skills are the same. What differs is who owns the account the clip is posted on, and how the clipper gets paid.

## How do clippers get paid?

There are two main models. Many clippers try both before settling on one.

### 1. Fixed pay: per clip, per batch, or monthly

A creator hires the clipper, agrees on what they need, and pays for the finished work. The clips go on the **creator's** channels. Typical 2026 rates:

| Work | Typical pay |
|---|---|
| Simple clip (talking head, podcast, captions) | $15 to $35 per clip |
| Mid-level clip (B-roll, zooms, branded captions) | $30 to $50 per clip |
| Complex clip (gaming sound effects, memes, motion) | $50 to $75+ per clip |
| Monthly retainer | About $800 to $3,500+ |

Example: a podcast pays $1,200 a month for 12 clips a week. That is 48 clips, or $25 per clip. This is how clipping works on Kreatli Marketplace. The full breakdown is in [clipper pay rates](/guides/clipper-pay-rates).

### 2. CPM pay: per 1,000 views

A creator or brand funds a campaign. Clippers download the approved footage, post clips on **their own** accounts, and earn based on views. Campaigns commonly advertise about **$0.50 to $3 per 1,000 views**. Most also set a minimum number of views before a clip counts and a cap on how much one clip can earn.

Example: 10 clips at $1 per 1,000 views. Six get under the 2,000-view minimum and earn nothing. Three get 8,000 views each and earn $24 in total. One gets 120,000 views and earns $120. That makes $144 for 10 clips.

CPM work can pay well when a clip takes off, but income swings a lot from week to week. ## What is a clipper job?

A clipper job is any paid work making short clips from someone else's long content. You will see them listed in a few ways:

- **Freelance clipping jobs:** a streamer wants 20 Shorts a week from their VODs, or a podcast wants clips from every episode. You apply, agree on rates and deadlines, and deliver files.
- **Retainers:** the same work as an ongoing monthly deal. This is the most stable kind of clipper job.
- **Clipping campaigns:** open calls where anyone can post clips and get paid per view. There is no application, but there is also no guaranteed pay. See [what a clipping campaign is](/glossary/what-is-a-clipping-campaign).

## Is clipping a real job?

Yes. Creators, streamers, podcasters and brands really do pay people to make clips. Some clippers earn pocket money and some earn a full-time wage, depending on skill, volume and which model they use.

The boom in "get paid to clip" offers has also drawn scams. Be careful if anyone asks you to pay upfront, keeps asking for unpaid tests, or changes the payout rules after you post. Our guide [is clipping legit?](/guides/is-clipping-legit) has a checklist for vetting any offer.

## How to become a clipper

You do not need a degree or expensive gear. The short version:

1. **Learn the basics:** cutting, reframing to 9:16, captions and hooks.
2. **Pick your software.** CapCut is free and enough to start.
3. **Choose a niche,** such as gaming, podcasts, streams or fitness.
4. **Make 5 to 10 sample clips** from public content in that niche.
5. **Find paid work:** apply to clipping jobs, join a few campaigns carefully, or contact creators directly.

## FAQ

### What does a clipper do?

A clipper finds the best moments in long videos, such as streams, podcasts and YouTube uploads, and edits them into short vertical clips for TikTok, Shorts and Reels. That means cutting, reframing to 9:16, adding captions and making sure the first seconds hook the viewer.

### Do clippers get paid?

Yes. Freelance clippers usually earn $15 to $35 per simple clip and $50 to $75 or more for complex work, or a monthly retainer. Clippers in CPM campaigns earn per 1,000 views on their own posts, commonly $0.50 to $3 per 1,000, and only on clips that pass the campaign's view minimum.

### What is a clipper job?

It is paid work making short clips from someone else's long content. It can be a one-off freelance project, an ongoing monthly retainer with a creator, or a CPM campaign where you post clips on your own account and earn from views.

### Is a clipper the same as a video editor?

A clipper is a kind of video editor. General editors may work on long YouTube videos, ads or films. Clippers focus only on short-form output: vertical framing, hooks, captions and fast pacing, usually cut from longer content.

### Do clippers post the clips themselves?

It depends on the model. Freelance clippers send finished files to the creator, who posts them on their own channels. That is how Kreatli Marketplace works. In CPM campaigns, clippers post on their own accounts so their views can be counted.

### Do you need experience to become a clipper?

No. Many clippers start with free software and a handful of sample clips. A small portfolio in one niche is usually enough to land a first paid job.
