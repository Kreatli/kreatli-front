# L-Cut and J-Cut Explained

An **L-cut** and a **J-cut** are edits where the audio and video change at different times. In an L-cut, the audio from one shot keeps playing after the picture cuts to the next shot. In a J-cut, the audio from the next shot starts before its picture appears. **L-cut J-cut editing** makes conversations and transitions feel smoother because the sound bridges the cut.

Both techniques show up constantly in podcast clips, interviews, and talking-head edits made by clippers on **Kreatli Marketplace**.

## Why the names

The names come from the shape the edit makes on a timeline, with video on the top track and audio on the bottom.

- **L-cut:** the audio from clip A extends past the video cut, so the clip looks like the letter L.
- **J-cut:** the audio from clip B starts before the video cut, so the clip looks like the letter J.

```
L-cut                         J-cut
Video: [ A ][ B      ]        Video: [ A      ][ B ]
Audio: [ A      ][ B ]        Audio: [ A ][ B      ]
```

## Why it matters for short-form clips

A hard cut switches picture and sound at the same moment. That works fine for many edits, but in conversation it can feel abrupt, especially when cutting between two speakers. Offsetting the audio makes the edit feel more natural.

For **clippers**, they are a simple way to make a podcast or interview clip look more polished without adding effects.

They help with:

- **Smoother speaker switches** in two-person podcasts and interviews
- **Showing reactions** while someone else is still talking
- **Hiding cuts** in a [talking-head edit](/glossary/talking-head-edit), since the voice carries over the change in picture
- **Better transitions** into B-roll or gameplay without breaking the sentence

## How L-cuts are used

An L-cut keeps the current audio running while the picture moves on. Common uses:

- **Reaction shots.** The guest keeps talking while the picture cuts to the host nodding or laughing.
- **B-roll over speech.** The speaker's sentence continues while the picture shows what they are describing.
- **Letting a line land.** The end of a punchline plays over the next shot, so the pacing does not feel chopped off.

## How J-cuts are used

A J-cut lets viewers hear the next shot before they see it. Common uses:

- **Introducing the next speaker.** The guest's answer starts while the picture still shows the host finishing the question.
- **Pulling into a new scene.** Sound from the next segment, such as crowd noise or a game start, begins early to set it up.
- **Hooks.** A clip can open with a voice line over a related visual, then cut to the speaker. Done carefully, this can help with the [first 3 seconds of a video](/glossary/video-hook-first-3-seconds).

## L-cut and J-cut vs other edits

| Technique | What changes | Common use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hard cut | Audio and video change together | Most basic edits |
| [Jump cut](/glossary/jump-cut-definition) | Time removed, same angle | Tightening talking-head clips |
| L-cut | Audio trails the video cut | Reactions, B-roll over speech |
| J-cut | Audio leads the video cut | Speaker switches, scene changes |

In short-form, editors often combine them. A podcast clip might use jump cuts to remove pauses, J-cuts to switch to the guest, and L-cuts to hold on a reaction.

## Examples

**Podcast clip:** The host asks a question, and the guest's answer starts while the camera is still on the host. The picture switches to the guest half a second later. That is a J-cut, and the switch feels like natural conversation.

**Interview clip:** The guest finishes a surprising answer. The audio continues as the picture cuts to the host's reaction. That is an L-cut, and it shows the moment landed.

## Tips for clippers

- **Keep offsets short.** In fast vertical clips, a few frames to half a second is often enough.
- **Watch captions.** [Burned-in captions](/glossary/burned-in-captions-meaning) should follow the audio, not the picture, so the right words appear while each person is speaking.
- **Check sync after reframing.** Cropping to vertical can change which face is on screen, so make sure the person viewers see matches what they hear when it matters.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between an L-cut and a J-cut?

In an L-cut, the audio from the first shot continues after the picture changes. In a J-cut, the audio from the next shot starts before the picture changes. Both overlap sound across a cut to make it feel smoother.

### Are L-cuts and J-cuts used in short-form video?

Yes, especially in podcast clips, interviews, and multi-speaker edits. They are less common in fast solo clips, where jump cuts do most of the work, but they help any clip with more than one person or angle.

### Can you make L-cuts and J-cuts in CapCut or Premiere Pro?

Yes. Most editors let you unlink or split audio from video, then extend the audio past the cut or start it earlier. The exact steps vary by app, but the idea is the same everywhere.
